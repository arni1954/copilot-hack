## 1. CORE CLASSICAL FORMULA LIBRARY

### Tonifying Formulas (Bu Ji 補劑)

**Si Jun Zi Tang (Four Gentlemen Decoction)**
- Pattern: Spleen/Stomach Qi deficiency
- Composition: Ren Shen (or Dang Shen), Bai Zhu, Fu Ling, Zhi Gan Cao
- Base formula for all Qi tonics — vast modification range
- Modern: Improves gastrointestinal function; immunomodulatory; adaptogenic

**Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang (Tonify Middle, Augment Qi Decoction)**
- Pattern: Spleen Qi deficiency with sinking; Qi deficiency fever (feverish from deficiency)
- Composition: Huang Qi, Ren Shen, Bai Zhu, Zhi Gan Cao, Dang Gui, Chen Pi, Sheng Ma, Chai Hu
- Key herb: Sheng Ma and Chai Hu — the lifting action that distinguishes this formula
- Clinical: Prolapse (uterine, rectal, gastric), chronic fatigue, orthostatic symptoms, postpartum weakness

**Si Wu Tang (Four Substances Decoction)**
- Pattern: Liver Blood deficiency
- Composition: Shu Di Huang, Dang Gui, Bai Shao, Chuan Xiong
- Base formula for all Blood tonics and gynaecological formulas
- Note: Chuan Xiong moves Blood — the formula tonifies AND moves, preventing stagnation from over-tonification

**Gui Pi Tang (Restore the Spleen Decoction)**
- Pattern: Spleen and Heart Blood/Qi deficiency; Spleen not governing Blood
- Composition: Si Jun Zi + Long Yan Rou, Suan Zao Ren, Yuan Zhi, Mu Xiang, Dang Gui, Huang Qi, Sheng Jiang, Da Zao
- Clinical: Insomnia with fatigue, poor memory, anxiety, heavy periods, chronic bleeding in deficiency context

**Ba Zhen Tang (Eight Treasure Decoction)**
- Pattern: Qi and Blood deficiency
- Composition: Si Jun Zi Tang + Si Wu Tang
- Post-illness, post-surgical, cancer recovery support

**Liu Wei Di Huang Wan (Six Ingredient Pill with Rehmannia)**
- Pattern: Kidney and Liver Yin deficiency
- Composition: Shu Di Huang, Shan Zhu Yu, Shan Yao, Ze Xie, Mu Dan Pi, Fu Ling
- Foundation formula: Dozens of variations built on this base
- Key modifications:
  - + Zhi Mu, Huang Bai → Zhi Bai Di Huang Wan (Empty Heat prominent)
  - + Gou Qi Zi, Ju Hua → Qi Ju Di Huang Wan (eye symptoms, Liver Yang rising)
  - + Wu Wei Zi, Mai Dong → Mai Wei Di Huang Wan (Lung Yin deficiency concurrent)

**Jin Gui Shen Qi Wan (Kidney Qi Pill from Golden Cabinet)**
- Pattern: Kidney Yang deficiency
- Composition: Liu Wei Di Huang Wan base + Gui Zhi (or Rou Gui), Fu Zi
- The Yin base with small Yang supplement: "A little fire ignites a greater fire"
- Distinguished from You Gui Wan which is pure Yang tonification

**You Gui Wan (Restore the Right Kidney Pill)**
- Pattern: Kidney Yang deficiency — severe; Ming Men Fire depleted
- Composition: Fu Zi, Rou Gui, Lu Jiao Jiao, Shu Di Huang, Shan Zhu Yu, Shan Yao, Gou Qi Zi, Tu Si Zi, Du Zhong, Dang Gui
- Heavy Yang tonification; for severe deficiency without concurrent excess

**Zuo Gui Wan (Restore the Left Kidney Pill)**
- Pattern: Kidney Yin and Jing deficiency — severe
- Composition: Shu Di Huang, Shan Yao, Gou Qi Zi, Shan Zhu Yu, Chuan Niu Xi, Tu Si Zi, Lu Jiao Jiao, Gui Ban Jiao
- Note: No Ze Xie or Mu Dan Pi — this is pure tonification, not the balanced approach of Liu Wei

### Qi-Regulating Formulas (Li Qi Ji 理氣劑)

**Chai Hu Shu Gan San (Bupleurum Powder to Spread the Liver)**
- Pattern: Liver Qi stagnation affecting the Stomach
- Composition: Si Ni San + Chuan Xiong, Xiang Fu, Chen Pi
- Clinical: Hypochondriac pain, irritability, IBS (stress-related), irregular periods with breast distension

**Xiao Yao San (Free and Easy Wanderer)**
- Pattern: Liver Qi stagnation with Spleen Qi deficiency and Blood deficiency
- One of the most-prescribed formulas in modern clinical practice
- Composition: Chai Hu, Dang Gui, Bai Shao, Bai Zhu, Fu Ling, Zhi Gan Cao, Sheng Jiang, Bo He
- Modifications: + Mu Dan Pi, Zhi Zi → Dan Zhi Xiao Yao San (Heat signs present)
- Clinical: Depression, PMS, perimenopausal syndrome, IBS with emotional component, chronic fatigue with emotional overlay

**Ban Xia Hou Po Tang (Pinellia and Magnolia Bark Decoction)**
- Pattern: Liver Qi stagnation with Phlegm — "Plum Stone Qi"
- Composition: Ban Xia, Hou Po, Fu Ling, Sheng Jiang, Zi Su Ye
- Clinical: Globus hystericus, somatisation, throat tightness from emotional suppression, mild anxiety with chest oppression

### Heat-Clearing Formulas (Qing Re Ji 清熱劑)

**Huang Lian Jie Du Tang (Coptis Decoction to Relieve Toxicity)**
- Pattern: Excess Fire-Toxin throughout three warmers
- Composition: Huang Lian, Huang Qin, Huang Bai, Zhi Zi
- Clinical: Severe infections with high fever, severe inflammatory conditions — adjunct only, alongside biomedical treatment. **Suspected sepsis is a medical emergency: refer immediately.**
- Caution: Bitter cold — damages Stomach; do not use long-term in Spleen deficiency

**Long Dan Xie Gan Tang (Gentiana Drain the Liver Decoction)**
- Pattern: Liver/Gallbladder Damp-Heat OR Liver Fire
- Composition: Long Dan Cao, Huang Qin, Zhi Zi, Mu Tong, Ze Xie, Che Qian Zi, Sheng Di, Dang Gui, Chai Hu, Zhi Gan Cao
- Clinical: Headache (temporal), tinnitus (acute loud), herpes zoster (shingles), UTI (Damp-Heat), vaginal discharge (yellow), acute conjunctivitis
- Caution: Not for long-term use — depleting; Mu Tong (Aristolochia concerns — use Tong Cao or Chuan Mu Tong)

**Bai Hu Tang (White Tiger Decoction)**
- Pattern: Yang Ming (Qi level) Heat — the "four bigs"
- Composition: Shi Gao, Zhi Mu, Jing Mi, Zhi Gan Cao
- Clinical: High fever, profuse sweating, intense thirst, rapid large pulse (acute febrile, diabetic Heat patterns)

**Yin Qiao San (Honeysuckle and Forsythia Powder)**
- Pattern: Wind-Heat exterior; early febrile illness — Wei level warm disease
- Composition: Jin Yin Hua, Lian Qiao, Bo He, Jie Geng, Niu Bang Zi, Dan Dou Chi, Jing Jie Sui, Dan Zhu Ye, Lu Gen, Zhi Gan Cao
- Clinical: Common cold/flu (heat-type), early tonsillitis, early otitis media, early COVID respiratory symptoms (evidence limited; not a substitute for testing or biomedical care)

### Blood-Regulating Formulas (Li Xue Ji 理血劑)

**Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang (Drive Out Stasis in the Mansion of Blood)**
- Pattern: Blood stasis in chest
- Wang Qing Ren's signature formula; contains Si Wu Tang + Chai Hu Shu Gan structure + Tao Hong
- Clinical: Chronic headache (fixed), chronic chest pain, depression with stabbing qualities, insomnia from stasis, post-traumatic pain

**Tao Hong Si Wu Tang (Si Wu Tang with Persica and Carthamus)**
- Pattern: Blood deficiency with Blood stasis
- Si Wu Tang + Tao Ren, Hong Hua
- Clinical: Dysmenorrhoea (blood clots, dark blood), amenorrhoea, fibroids, irregular menstruation — very common gynaecological prescription

**Shao Fu Zhu Yu Tang (Drive Out Stasis from the Lower Abdomen)**
- Pattern: Blood stasis and Cold in Lower Jiao
- Clinical: Endometriosis (cold-type), chronic pelvic pain, dysmenorrhoea (cold, clots, relieved by heat)

### Phlegm-Resolving Formulas

**Er Chen Tang (Two Cured Decoction)**
- Base formula for all Phlegm-Damp patterns
- Composition: Ban Xia, Chen Pi, Fu Ling, Zhi Gan Cao, Sheng Jiang, Wu Mei
- Foundation for dozens of modifications

**Wen Dan Tang (Warm the Gallbladder Decoction)**
- Pattern: Gallbladder and Stomach Phlegm-Heat; Phlegm disturbing Heart Shen
- Composition: Er Chen Tang + Zhi Shi, Zhu Ru
- Clinical: Insomnia (Phlegm-Heat), anxiety with phlegm signs, nausea/vomiting, dizziness (phlegm-type), depression with phlegm obstruction

**Ban Xia Bai Zhu Tian Ma Tang (Pinellia, Atractylodis, and Gastrodia Decoction)**
- Pattern: Wind-Phlegm — Phlegm obstructing, generating internal Wind
- Clinical: Dizziness (Phlegm-type — worse movement, nausea, greasy coat), Ménière's disease pattern, vertigo

### Mind-Calming Formulas (An Shen Ji 安神劑)

**Suan Zao Ren Tang (Sour Jujube Decoction)**
- Pattern: Liver Blood deficiency failing to nourish Heart; deficiency-type insomnia
- Composition: Suan Zao Ren, Fu Ling, Zhi Mu, Chuan Xiong, Zhi Gan Cao
- Clinical: Insomnia (difficulty staying asleep, dream-disturbed), irritability with fatigue, perimenopausal insomnia

**Tian Wang Bu Xin Dan (Emperor of Heaven's Special Pill to Tonify the Heart)**
- Pattern: Heart and Kidney Yin deficiency; Yin unable to anchor Yang/Shen
- Clinical: Insomnia (difficulty falling asleep with agitation), palpitations, poor memory/concentration, night sweats, dry mouth — classic for knowledge workers, menopausal pattern


### Interior-Warming Formulas (Wen Li Ji 溫裏劑)

**Li Zhong Wan (Regulate the Middle Pill)**
- Pattern: Spleen-Stomach deficiency Cold; Middle Jiao Yang insufficiency
- Composition: Ren Shen, Gan Jiang, Bai Zhu, Zhi Gan Cao
- The foundational Middle Jiao warming formula; basis for dozens of modifications
- Clinical: Chronic diarrhoea (cold type), vomiting clear fluid, abdominal cold pain, poor appetite with cold sensation

**Fu Zi Li Zhong Wan (Aconite Regulate the Middle)**
- Li Zhong Wan + Fu Zi — for severe Spleen-Kidney Yang deficiency
- Clinical: Dawn diarrhoea, cold extremities, profound fatigue with cold

**Wu Zhu Yu Tang (Evodia Decoction)**
- Pattern: Deficiency Cold in Liver and Stomach; Jue Yin Cold headache
- Composition: Wu Zhu Yu, Ren Shen, Sheng Jiang, Da Zao
- Clinical: Vertex headache with cold hands, nausea/vomiting (especially with headache), acid reflux (cold type)

**Xiao Jian Zhong Tang (Minor Construct the Middle Decoction)**
- Pattern: Middle Jiao deficiency with abdominal spasm/pain; Ying-Wei disharmony
- Composition: Gui Zhi, Bai Shao (doubled), Yi Tang (maltose), Sheng Jiang, Da Zao, Zhi Gan Cao
- Clinical: Abdominal cramping pain (worse cold, better warmth and pressure), palpitations, fatigue — classic for children and constitutional Spleen weakness

**Dang Gui Si Ni Tang (Tangkuei Decoction for Frigid Extremities)**
- Pattern: Cold Obstruction in channels — Xue (Blood) Cold; Cold hands and feet from Blood deficiency + Cold invasion
- Composition: Dang Gui, Gui Zhi, Bai Shao, Xi Xin, Tong Cao, Da Zao, Zhi Gan Cao
- Caution: Xi Xin — aristolochic acid concern and classical dose ceiling; see §4 Toxic / Restricted Substances
- Clinical: Ice-cold extremities (especially fingers), Raynaud's syndrome (Cold-Blood type), frostbite sequelae, cold-type dysmenorrhoea

### Exterior-Releasing Formulas (Jie Biao Ji 解表劑)

*Already partially covered under Wind-Cold/Wind-Heat in Lung patterns. Key additional formulas:*

**Jiu Wei Qiang Huo Tang (Nine-Herb Decoction with Notopterygium)**
- Pattern: Wind-Cold-Damp exterior; whole body aches dominant
- Useful when body aches, heaviness, and limb pain are the leading symptoms
- Formula: Qiang Huo, Fang Feng, Cang Zhu + clearing herbs

**Chai Ge Jie Ji Tang (Bupleurum and Kudzu Decoction to Release the Muscle Layer)**
- Pattern: Exterior Cold beginning to transform Heat; Tai Yang–Yang Ming crossover
- Clinical: Cold/flu with initial Cold now showing Heat signs — lingering fever, stiff neck, irritability

**Xin Yi San (Magnolia Flower Powder)**
- Pattern: Wind-Cold obstructing nasal passages
- Clinical: Chronic rhinitis, sinusitis, blocked nose — specific to nasal pathway

### Astringent/Consolidating Formulas (Gu Se Ji 固澀劑)

**Jin Suo Gu Jing Wan (Metal Lock Pill to Stabilise the Essence)**
- Pattern: Kidney Qi not firm — spermatorrhoea, premature ejaculation
- Composition: Sha Yuan Ji Li, Qian Shi, Lian Xu, Long Gu, Mu Li, Lian Zi
- Clinical: Spermatorrhoea, nocturnal emissions, premature ejaculation in Kidney deficiency context

**Sang Piao Xiao San (Mantis Egg Case Powder)**
- Pattern: Heart and Kidney not communicating; Kidney Qi deficiency
- Clinical: Frequent urination (many times per day, clear and copious), enuresis, spermatorrhoea, poor memory, anxiety — the pattern where urinary incontinence + mental unrest coexist

**Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang** *(already listed — consolidates Qi to prevent prolapse/sinking)*

**Wan Dai Tang (End Discharge Decoction)**
- Pattern: Spleen deficiency with Damp — white vaginal discharge
- Composition: Bai Zhu, Shan Yao, Ren Shen, Bai Shao, Che Qian Zi, Cang Zhu, Gan Cao, Chen Pi, Jing Jie Sui, Chai Hu
- Clinical: Profuse white vaginal discharge, fatigue, loose stools, Spleen Qi deficiency presentation

**Zhen Ren Yang Zang Tang (True Man's Decoction that Nourishes the Organs)**
- Pattern: Spleen-Kidney Yang deficiency — chronic diarrhoea/dysentery; intestinal Qi not consolidated
- Clinical: Chronic dysentery, chronic diarrhoea with prolapse, incontinence of stool

### Orifice-Opening Formulas (Kai Qiao Ji 開竅劑)

*Used for emergency unconsciousness — Closed vs Open patterns. Critical distinction: Closed (Bi 閉) = forceful opening needed; Open (Tuo 脫) = do NOT open, needs rescue of Yang.*

**An Gong Niu Huang Wan (Calm the Palace Pill with Cattle Gallstone)**
- Pattern: Hot Closed type — Phlegm-Heat blocking Heart orifices
- Clinical: High fever with unconsciousness, delirium, convulsions, hot skin, red face
- Historical / in-hospital use (China): Stroke (ischaemic, early), encephalitis, meningitis (Heat type) — **never a substitute for emergency care; these presentations go to emergency services first**
- **TOXICITY: contains Zhu Sha (cinnabar — mercury) and Xiong Huang (realgar — arsenic).** See §4 Toxic / Restricted Substances.

**Su He Xiang Wan (Liquid Styrax Pill)**
- Pattern: Cold Closed type — Cold-Phlegm blocking Heart orifices
- Clinical: Sudden loss of consciousness with cold limbs, pale face, trismus
- Historical / in-hospital use (China): Cold-type stroke, cardiac events with cold presentation — **never a substitute for emergency care**

**Zi Xue Dan (Purple Snow Elixir)**
- Pattern: High fever, convulsions, delirium — extreme Heat stirring Wind, threatening orifices
- Clinical: Severe febrile illness with neurological involvement (in-hospital context only)
- **TOXICITY: contains Zhu Sha (cinnabar — mercury) among other mineral ingredients.** See §4 Toxic / Restricted Substances.

**Clinical rule:** Closed (Bi 閉) = strong pulse, clenched jaw, closed fists. Do not use tonics — open the orifices.
Collapse (Tuo) = weak pulse, open jaw, open hands, profuse sweat. Do NOT open — rescue Yang immediately with Shen Fu Tang (ginseng + aconite).

### Digestive/Food Stagnation Formulas (Xiao Dao Ji 消導劑)

**Bao He Wan (Preserve Harmony Pill)**
- Pattern: Food stagnation in Stomach — the primary mild food stagnation formula
- Composition: Shan Zha, Shen Qu, Ban Xia, Fu Ling, Chen Pi, Lian Qiao, Lai Fu Zi
- Clinical: Acute food stagnation, post-overindulgence, hiccough, belching, epigastric fullness
- Note: Each herb targets specific food type (Shan Zha — meat; Lai Fu Zi — grains; Shen Qu — alcohol/fermented)

**Zhi Shi Dao Zhi Wan (Unripe Bitter Orange Pill to Guide Out Stagnation)**
- Pattern: Food stagnation with Damp-Heat
- Clinical: Epigastric distension and pain, foul-smelling diarrhoea or constipation, tenesmus

**Jian Pi Wan (Strengthen the Spleen Pill)**
- Pattern: Chronic Spleen Qi deficiency with food stagnation — the deficiency + excess combination
- Clinical: Chronic digestive weakness where every meal causes discomfort; children's nutritional stagnation (Gan Ji 疳積)

## 2. FORMULA MODIFICATION PRINCIPLES

Classical formulas are starting points. Modifications (Jia Jian 加減) individualise treatment.

**Adding single herbs for symptoms:**
- Insomnia → add Suan Zao Ren, Ye Jiao Teng, He Huan Pi
- Anxiety → add Long Gu, Mu Li (anchor Shen)
- Constipation (dry) → add Huo Ma Ren, Yu Li Ren
- Constipation (cold) → add Rou Cong Rong
- Pain → add Yan Hu Suo, Chuan Lian Zi (if Heat-type Qi stagnation pain)
- Oedema → add Ze Xie, Yi Yi Ren, Che Qian Zi
- Heavy bleeding → add Ai Ye Tan, Pao Jiang (warming); or Di Yu, Huai Hua (Heat-type)
- Hot flushes → add Qing Hao, Di Gu Pi (Clear deficiency Heat)

**Adjusting thermal nature:**
- Formula too cold for constitution → add Gan Jiang, Rou Gui
- Formula too warming for Heat signs → add Sheng Di, Mai Dong

## 3. KEY MATERIA MEDICA — ESSENTIAL HERBS

### Tonifying Herbs

**Ren Shen (Ginseng)** — Sweet, slightly bitter; warm
- Functions: Strongly tonifies Yuan (Source) Qi; tonifies Lung and Spleen; generates fluids; calms Shen
- Clinical: Qi deficiency collapse (emergency: large dose), chronic fatigue, immune deficiency
- Caution: Heat patterns; hypertension; do not use with strong stimulants
- Drug interaction: MAOIs; warfarin (mild); loop diuretics

**Huang Qi (Astragalus)** — Sweet; slightly warm
- Functions: Tonifies Spleen and Lung Qi; raises Yang (lifting); stabilises Wei Qi; diuretic
- Clinical: Chronic fatigue, immune deficiency, spontaneous sweating, prolapse, oedema
- Evidence: Strong immunomodulatory data; enhances NK cell activity; used in cancer support
- Drug interaction: Immunosuppressants (theoretical antagonism); cyclophosphamide (may potentiate)

**Dang Gui (Angelica sinensis)** — Sweet, acrid; warm
- Functions: Tonifies Blood; activates Blood; regulates menstruation; moistens intestines
- Clinical: Blood deficiency, menstrual irregularity, anaemia support, constipation (dry-type)
- Drug interaction: **Warfarin (significant — increases anticoagulant effect)**; antiplatelet agents
- Caution: Diarrhoea; pregnancy (large doses)

**Shu Di Huang (Prepared Rehmannia)** — Sweet; slightly warm
- Functions: Tonifies Yin, nourishes Blood, supplements Jing; enriches Kidney and Liver
- Clinical: Yin and Blood deficiency — the fundamental nourishing herb
- Caution: Greasy, difficult to digest — add Chen Pi or Sha Ren if Spleen deficiency

**He Shou Wu (Polygonum multiflorum)** — Bitter, sweet, astringent; slightly warm
- Functions: Tonifies Liver and Kidney; nourishes Blood; secures Jing; anti-ageing
- **SAFETY WARNING: Hepatotoxicity risk — cases of severe liver injury documented. Use under qualified practitioner supervision. Do not use in known liver disease. Monitor LFTs in prolonged use.**

### Heat-Clearing Herbs

**Huang Lian (Coptis)** — Bitter; cold
- Functions: Drains Heart Fire; dries Damp-Heat (Middle and Lower Jiao); detoxifies
- Evidence: Berberine — significant anti-diabetic evidence (insulin sensitiser); antimicrobial
- Drug interaction: Metformin (additive); cyclosporine; digoxin

**Huang Qin (Scutellaria baicalensis)** — Bitter; cold
- Functions: Drains Fire; dries Damp; clears Heat-Toxin; calms fetus
- Distinguished from Huang Qi (Astragalus) — completely different herb despite similar pinyin

### Moving/Activating Herbs

**Chai Hu (Bupleurum)** — Bitter, acrid; cool
- Functions: Spreads Liver Qi; raises Yang Qi; clears Shao Yang Heat; regulates Liver-Spleen
- Drug interaction: Hepatotoxicity in large doses (rare); enzyme inducer

**Chuan Xiong (Ligusticum)** — Acrid; warm
- Functions: Activates Blood; promotes Qi movement; expels Wind; stops pain
- The herb that makes Blood-tonifying formulas therapeutically dynamic rather than stagnating

---

### Blood-Activating and Stasis-Resolving Herbs

**Dan Shen (Salvia miltiorrhiza)** — Bitter; slightly cold
- Functions: Activates Blood; removes stasis; cools Blood; calms Shen
- **CRITICAL drug interaction: Warfarin — significantly potentiates anticoagulation. Near-contraindicated concurrent use.**
- Modern: Tanshinones → NF-κB inhibition; cardioprotective; hepatoprotective; neuroprotective; strong clinical evidence for cardiovascular disease
- Clinical: Coronary artery disease (significant evidence), angina, hepatic fibrosis, insomnia from Blood stasis/Heat

**San Qi / Tian Qi (Panax notoginseng)** — Sweet, slightly bitter; warm
- Functions: Activates Blood; stops bleeding simultaneously — the only herb that does both
- The classical formula Yunnan Baiyao contains San Qi as primary ingredient
- Modern: Notoginsenoside R1 → platelet aggregation inhibition; haemostatic; anti-inflammatory; cardioprotective
- Clinical: Traumatic bleeding, coronary artery disease, menstrual Blood stasis with concurrent bleeding risk
- Drug interaction: Warfarin (additive anticoagulation); antiplatelet agents

**Yan Hu Suo (Corydalis)** — Acrid, bitter; warm
- Functions: Activates Blood; moves Qi; stops pain
- The most important analgesic herb in TCM — crosses all types of pain
- Modern: l-Tetrahydropalmatine → dopamine receptor antagonism; sedative/analgesic; GABA-A modulation
- Clinical: Any pain (menstrual, abdominal, traumatic, headache); insomnia from pain; drug interaction potential with CNS depressants

**Chuan Niu Xi (Cyathula root)** — Bitter, sour; neutral
- Functions: Activates Blood; tonifies Liver and Kidney; guides Blood and Qi downward; strengthens sinews and bones
- The "guiding" function — directs formula action to lower body (lower back, legs, genitals, joints)
- Clinical: Lower body Blood stasis pain; urinary blood stasis; hypertension (guides Yang down); joint pain (lower body)

### Wind-Expelling Herbs

**Tian Ma (Gastrodia)** — Sweet; neutral
- Functions: Extinguishes Liver Wind; calms Yang; stops pain; resolves Phlegm
- Primary herb for internal Wind — dizziness, tremors, headache, convulsions
- Modern: Gastrodin → neuroprotective; anti-epileptic; reduces blood pressure; vasodilatory
- Clinical: Dizziness, Liver Yang rising, Wind-Phlegm headache, essential tremor, epilepsy support

**Gou Teng (Uncaria)** — Sweet; cool
- Functions: Extinguishes Wind; clears Heat; calms Liver Yang
- Usually combined with Tian Ma (Tian Ma Gou Teng Yin — the primary hypertension formula in TCM)
- Modern: Rhynchophylline → calcium channel blockade; anti-hypertensive; neuroprotective

**Quan Xie (Scorpion) / Wu Gong (Centipede)**
- Animal herbs for Wind — used only in severe internal Wind with convulsions, facial paralysis, chronic intractable pain
- Strong toxicity profile — use under qualified supervision only; dose carefully

### Qi-Moving Herbs (Key Individuals)

**Xiang Fu (Cyperus)** — Acrid, slightly bitter, slightly sweet; neutral
- "The chief herb for regulating Qi in the Liver channel" — Li Shizhen
- Functions: Spreads Liver Qi; regulates menstruation; stops pain
- Clinical: Liver Qi stagnation with menstrual irregularity; emotional pain; hypochondriac pain; the most important Qi-moving herb for gynaecology

**Chen Pi (Tangerine Peel)** — Acrid, bitter; warm
- Functions: Regulates Qi; transforms Phlegm; dries Damp; harmonises Middle Jiao
- Present in almost every digestive formula — the harmonising Qi-moving herb
- Prevents cloying tonics from stagnating (added to formulas with Shu Di Huang)

**Zhi Shi (Immature Bitter Orange)** — Bitter, acrid; slightly cold
- Functions: Breaks Qi stagnation; reduces accumulation; transforms Phlegm
- Stronger action than Chen Pi — breaks rather than moves
- Clinical: Food stagnation, constipation (Qi stagnation type), prolapse (paradoxically — downward-moving can lift in formula context with appropriate combinations)

## 4. HERB-DRUG INTERACTIONS — CRITICAL SAFETY TABLE

| Herb | Drug Class | Interaction | Clinical Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dang Gui, Dan Shen | Warfarin, antiplatelet | Enhanced anticoagulation | HIGH — monitor INR |
| Dan Shen (Salvia) | Warfarin | Dramatically increased bleeding risk | HIGH — contraindicated |
| Gan Cao (licorice) | Antihypertensives, corticosteroids | Pseudohyperaldosteronism; drug level changes | MODERATE |
| Ma Huang (Ephedra) | MAOIs, sympathomimetics, cardiac drugs | Hypertensive crisis; arrhythmia | HIGH — avoid |
| He Shou Wu | Any hepatotoxic drugs | Additive liver toxicity | HIGH — monitor LFTs |
| Ren Shen (Ginseng) | MAOIs, warfarin, hypoglycaemics | Multiple interactions | MODERATE — disclose |
| Huang Lian (Berberine) | Metformin, cyclosporine | Altered pharmacokinetics | MODERATE |
| St John's Wort (Western but crosses systems) | Many drugs (CYP3A4 inducer) | Reduces drug levels | HIGH — major interactions |
| Chan Su (cardiac glycoside source) | Digoxin | Additive cardiac effects | HIGH — dangerous |
| Gou Qi Zi (Lycium) | Warfarin | Raised INR (case reports) | MODERATE — monitor INR |

**Always ask about all concurrent medications before prescribing herbs.**

### Toxic / Restricted Substances — Critical Safety

**Fu Zi (Aconitum carmichaelii, lateral root)** — Acrid, sweet; very hot; **toxic**
- Present in: Jin Gui Shen Qi Wan, You Gui Wan, Fu Zi Li Zhong Wan, Zhen Wu Tang, Shen Fu Tang, Si Ni Tang
- Toxicity: Aconitine alkaloids — cardiotoxic and neurotoxic. Signs: perioral/limb numbness and tingling, nausea, vomiting, hypotension, bradycardia or ventricular arrhythmia; fatal cases documented
- Rules: Processed form only (Zhi Fu Zi / Pao Fu Zi) — never raw; pre-decoct (Xian Jian 先煎) before other herbs; contraindicated in pregnancy; avoid in Heat patterns and Yin deficiency with Heat; caution with cardiac disease and antiarrhythmics
- Classical incompatibility (Shi Ba Fan 十八反): Wu Tou (aconite) opposes Ban Xia, Gua Lou, Bei Mu, Bai Lian, Bai Ji
- Recommend only under qualified practitioner supervision

**Zhu Sha (Cinnabar — mercuric sulphide) and Xiong Huang (Realgar — arsenic sulphide)**
- Present in: An Gong Niu Huang Wan (both), Zi Xue Dan (Zhu Sha), and some calming/Shen-settling patent pills
- Risks: Mercury and arsenic accumulation — renal, hepatic and neurological toxicity
- Rules: Not for self-medication, long-term use, children, pregnancy or breastfeeding, or renal/hepatic impairment. Import and sale are restricted in many jurisdictions — check local regulation before recommending

**Aristolochic acid herbs**
- Guan Mu Tong (Aristolochia manshuriensis) and Guang Fang Ji (Aristolochia fangchi): nephrotoxic and carcinogenic (aristolochic acid nephropathy; urothelial cancer). **Do not use.** Substitute Chuan Mu Tong (Clematis) or Tong Cao (Tetrapanax) for Mu Tong; Han Fang Ji (Stephania) for Fang Ji — and verify species identity of the product
- Xi Xin (Asarum): contains aristolochic acid (concentrated in aerial parts — root only should be used). Classical dose ceiling: "Xi Xin bu guo qian" (細辛不過錢 — do not exceed one qian, ~3 g, especially in powder form). Avoid in renal disease and pregnancy

**Animal toxins (Quan Xie, Wu Gong, Chan Su)** — see entries above; supervised use only. Chan Su (toad venom) is a cardiac glycoside source — never with digoxin

## 5. MODERN PHARMACOLOGICAL CORRELATES

**Astragalus (Huang Qi):**
- Polysaccharides: TLR-4 agonism → innate immune activation
- Telomerase activation (TA-65 research) — Jing preservation parallel
- Anti-fatigue: Improves mitochondrial function in animal models

**Berberine (from Huang Lian, Huang Bai):**
- AMPK activation (same pathway as metformin) → insulin sensitisation
- Lipid lowering (inhibits PCSK9)
- Microbiome modulation
- Clinical trials: Comparable to metformin for T2DM lipid outcomes in some studies

**Shu Di Huang / Di Huang:**
- Iridoid glycosides (catalpol, aucubin) — neuroprotective; anti-inflammatory
- Blood glucose modulation

**Ren Shen (Ginseng) ginsenosides:**
- HPA axis modulation — adaptogenic
- Rb1: CNS depressant/neuroprotective
- Rg1: CNS stimulant/immunomodulatory
- NF-κB inhibition — anti-inflammatory

**Dang Gui ligustilide:**
- Smooth muscle relaxant — mechanism of action for dysmenorrhoea treatment
- Platelet aggregation inhibition — mechanism of anticoagulant interaction

**Chai Hu saikosaponins:**
- Anti-inflammatory (COX-2 and 5-LOX inhibition)
- Liver protective at low doses; hepatotoxic at high doses
- Corticosteroid-sparing potential

**Yin Chen Hao (Artemisia capillaris):**
- Strongly choleretic and hepatoprotective
- Used in viral hepatitis protocols — some clinical evidence
