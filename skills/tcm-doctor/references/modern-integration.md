## 1. INTEGRATION PHILOSOPHY

TCM and biomedicine are not in competition — they describe the same biological reality from different observational positions and through different conceptual frameworks. The integrative practitioner maintains fluency in both without reducing one to the other.

**The complementary lens model:**
- Biomedicine: mechanistic, reductive, excellent at acute intervention, pathogen identification, surgical/emergency
- TCM: pattern-based, systemic, temporal, relational, excellent at chronic conditions, constitutional optimisation, preventive medicine, psycho-emotional integration

**Where integration adds most value:**
1. Chronic complex conditions (autoimmune, metabolic, functional)
2. Cancer — adjunctive support (quality of life, side effect management)
3. Hormonal and reproductive medicine
4. Psycho-emotional/neuropsychiatric conditions
5. Conditions where biomedicine has no effective treatment or significant side effects
6. Preventive medicine and constitutional optimisation (longevity)

**Where biomedicine is primary:**
- Acute infections with systemic involvement
- Surgical emergencies
- Clearly defined pathogen-driven disease (bacterial infection, parasitic)
- Cancer (curative treatment — TCM as adjunct, not primary)
- Life-threatening conditions requiring rapid mechanical or pharmacological intervention

## 2. BIOMEDICAL CORRELATES OF TCM CONCEPTS

### Qi
**Not:** Mystical life force (this framing impedes clinical thinking)
**More accurately:** A systems-level concept encompassing multiple biomedical phenomena:

| Qi Type | Biomedical Parallel |
|---|---|
| Yuan Qi (Source Qi) | Mitochondrial ATP production; constitutional genetic/epigenetic baseline |
| Wei Qi (Defensive Qi) | Innate immune function; skin barrier; mucosal immunity |
| Ying Qi (Nutritive Qi) | Post-absorptive nutrition metabolism; blood glucose regulation |
| Zhong Qi (Central Qi) | Respiratory musculature; cardiac contractility; diaphragmatic function |
| Qi of specific organs | Organ-specific function — Lung Qi = respiratory capacity; Spleen Qi = digestive enzyme production + motility |

**Qi Deficiency** at different levels maps to:
- Mitochondrial dysfunction → fatigue
- Autonomic nervous system dysregulation → postural symptoms, sweating dysregulation
- Immunodeficiency → recurrent infections
- Smooth muscle hypofunction → digestive motility issues, uterine atony

### Blood (Xue)
- **Blood Deficiency:** Not purely anaemia (though that's one presentation) — encompasses: iron deficiency (without frank anaemia), B12/folate deficiency, poor erythropoiesis, insufficient Blood-nourishing nutrients (protein, fats), reduced peripheral circulation
- **Blood Stasis:** Hypercoagulability, increased platelet aggregation, poor microcirculation, chronic inflammation with vascular involvement, structural vascular pathology
- **Blood Heat:** Inflammatory endothelial activation; elevated inflammatory cytokines; vasodilation and increased vascular permeability

### Yin/Yang
- **Yin Deficiency:** Often correlates with: parasympathetic insufficiency, HPA axis hyperactivity (chronic stress → cortisol depletion → adrenal fatigue), sex hormone decline (especially oestrogen), thyroid T3 activity changes, reduced anabolic hormones
- **Yang Deficiency:** Often correlates with: hypothyroidism, adrenal insufficiency, autonomic dysregulation (sympathetic deficit), testosterone deficiency (men), insulin resistance with metabolic slowdown, mitochondrial cold sensitivity

### Kidney System
The biomedical breadth of the Kidney system is striking:

| TCM Kidney Function | Biomedical System |
|---|---|
| Governs Water metabolism | Renal → aldosterone → fluid regulation |
| Stores Jing | Genetic material + epigenetic programming; reproductive endocrine axis |
| Controls bones and marrow | Bone metabolism (osteocalcin, PTH, Vit D); bone marrow haematopoiesis |
| Opens to ears | Cochlear microcirculation; auditory nerve function |
| Governs reproduction | HPG axis: GnRH, LH, FSH, testosterone, oestrogen, progesterone |
| Controls inhalation (grasping Qi) | Respiratory diaphragm, phrenic nerve, respiratory centre in brainstem |
| Root of Ming Men Fire | Mitochondrial energy production; core body temperature regulation |

**Kidney Yin Deficiency → HPA axis dysregulation model:**
Chronic stress → cortisol → adrenal over-activation → eventual depletion → low DHEA, low morning cortisol, poor sleep architecture, night sweats, dry mouth, tinnitus (inner ear microcirculation) — classic Kidney Yin deficiency presentation

### Liver System
| TCM Liver Function | Biomedical System |
|---|---|
| Ensures smooth flow of Qi | Autonomic nervous system regulation; smooth muscle tone throughout body |
| Stores Blood | Hepatic blood storage; coagulation factor production |
| Controls tendons | Collagen metabolism; musculotendinous unit function |
| Opens to eyes | Hepatic bile salt involvement in fat-soluble vitamin absorption (Vit A → vision) |
| Governs emotions (anger) | HPA axis; amygdala regulation; serotonin/dopamine balance |

**Liver Qi Stagnation → autonomic dysregulation model:**
Psychosocial stress → sympathetic activation without discharge → smooth muscle tension throughout body (esophageal spasm, IBS, uterine cramps, tension headache) → secondary inflammatory cascade → Damp-Heat accumulation → more complex patterns

### Heart System (Xin 心)
| TCM Heart Function | Biomedical System |
|---|---|
| Governs Blood and vessels | Cardiac output; vascular tone; endothelial function; coagulation |
| Houses the Shen (spirit) | Prefrontal cortex-HPA axis integration; neurotransmitter balance (serotonin, dopamine); sleep architecture |
| Opens to the tongue | Cranial nerve XII/IX; sublingual mucosa vascularity (tongue diagnosis biomarker) |
| Manifests in the complexion | Peripheral vasodilation; capillary density; NO signalling |
| Controls sweat | Cholinergic sympathetic sweat glands; autonomic regulation |
| Pairs with Small Intestine | Gut-brain axis; serotonin (90% produced in gut); Small Intestine "separating pure from impure" = digestive enzyme action + gut barrier function |

**Heart Qi Deficiency → cardiac output / ANS correlate:**
Reduced cardiac contractility, decreased heart rate variability (HRV), autonomic dysregulation — HRV is the modern objective measure most closely correlating with Heart Qi strength in TCM terms. Low HRV = Heart Qi deficiency; Heart Yang deficiency in severe cases.

**Heart Blood deficiency → neurotransmitter / sleep architecture correlate:**
Reduced serotonin synthesis, poor sleep architecture (reduced slow-wave sleep), low ferritin (iron for neurotransmitter production), possible SSRI-responsive depression — Blood nourishment parallels providing substrate for neurotransmitter production.

**Heart Fire → sympathetic overdrive / inflammatory correlate:**
HPA axis hyperactivation; elevated cortisol; elevated CRP; sympathetic dominance; vagal withdrawal — the neuroinflammatory state that generates the "Heart Fire" clinical picture.

### Spleen System
| TCM Spleen Function | Biomedical System |
|---|---|
| Governs transformation and transportation | Digestive enzyme secretion; intestinal absorption; pancreatic function |
| Controls the muscles and flesh | Protein synthesis; muscle metabolic efficiency; sarcopenia prevention |
| Controls Blood (keeps in vessels) | Platelet function; vascular wall integrity; clotting cascade |
| Raises Qi (upward) | Colonic motility; pelvic floor integrity; ligamentous support |
| Opens to the mouth, manifests in lips | Oral health; taste perception; salivary gland function |

**Spleen Qi Deficiency → metabolic syndrome correlation:**
Poor digestive function → reduced nutrient extraction → mitochondrial insufficiency → fatigue → secondary Damp accumulation → insulin resistance → visceral adiposity → further Spleen compromise (the vicious cycle)

## 3. CONDITIONS — DUAL-SYSTEM ANALYSIS

### Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS)

**Biomedical:** Functional bowel disorder — visceral hypersensitivity, altered gut motility, gut-brain axis dysregulation, microbiome dysbiosis, possible post-infectious trigger, stress responsivity

**TCM lens (multiple possible patterns):**
- Liver Qi stagnation invading Spleen (most common in stress-related IBS): bloating, alternating bowel, irritability, wiry pulse
- Spleen Qi deficiency with Damp: fatigue, loose stools, food sensitivities, teeth-marked tongue
- Kidney Yang deficiency: dawn diarrhoea, cold abdomen, worse cold
- Damp-Heat in Large Intestine: urgency, burning, yellow/mucous stool

**Integration:** TCM adds differential precision that guides treatment when "IBS" is a single biomedical diagnosis masking multiple distinct physiological patterns. Biomedical testing (SIBO, colonoscopy, coeliac screen, stool microbiome) clarifies what TCM pattern differentiation may not.

**Evidence:** Certain formulas (Tong Xie Yao Fang for Liver-Spleen disharmony type; Si Shen Wan for Kidney Yang type) have RCT evidence for IBS symptom reduction.

### Menopausal Syndrome

**Biomedical:** Declining oestrogen and progesterone → vasomotor instability, sleep disruption, cognitive changes, bone loss, cardiovascular risk shift, urogenital atrophy, mood changes

**TCM lens:**
- Kidney Yin deficiency: most common — night sweats, hot flushes (afternoon/evening), dry vagina, insomnia (waking), tinnitus, lower back ache, red tongue no coat
- Kidney Yin and Yang deficiency: both hot and cold symptoms — instability
- Heart and Kidney not communicating: palpitations + insomnia + hot flushes
- Liver Qi stagnation + Kidney deficiency: mood instability, irritability, hot flushes — Fire from stagnation

**Integration:** Liu Wei Di Huang Wan or Zhi Bai Di Huang Wan for Kidney Yin deficiency type has good evidence. Biomedical HRT is not contraindicated alongside TCM; the two work well together.

**Evidence:** Systematic reviews show acupuncture modestly reduces hot flush frequency and improves sleep; herbal formulas show variable results depending on pattern match.

### Hypertension

**Biomedical:** Elevated systolic/diastolic BP → cardiovascular and renal risk. Mechanisms: increased cardiac output, increased peripheral vascular resistance, renin-angiotensin-aldosterone activation, sympathetic overactivation, endothelial dysfunction

**TCM lens:**
- Liver Yang Rising (most common in middle-aged stressed individuals): headache (temporal), tinnitus, red face, irritability, wiry rapid pulse
- Liver Fire: more severe — severe headache, anger, constipation, red eyes
- Phlegm-Damp obstruction: obesity, heaviness, muzzy head, greasy coat, slippery pulse
- Yin deficiency (Liver-Kidney): dizziness, dry eyes, night sweats, thin rapid pulse
- Blood stasis: fixed headache, purple tongue, choppy pulse (often in older patients)

**Integration:** Biomedical antihypertensives should continue — do NOT advise stopping medication. TCM addresses the underlying pattern while biomedical therapy controls the BP risk. Acupuncture (ST-36, SP-6, LV-3, LI-11, GB-20) has modest evidence for mild BP reduction. Tian Ma Gou Teng Yin formula: some RCT evidence for adjunctive BP reduction in Liver Yang rising pattern.

### Infertility (Female)

**Biomedical:** Multiple causes — ovulatory dysfunction (PCOS, POI), tubal factor, uterine factor (fibroids, polyps, adenomyosis), cervical factor, implantation failure, endometriosis, unexplained

**TCM lens:**
- Kidney deficiency (Jing): delayed puberty history, pale scanty periods, thin tongue body, weak pulse — constitutional deficiency
- Blood deficiency: scanty pale periods, pallor, fatigue, choppy pulse
- Blood stasis: dysmenorrhoea with clots, endometriosis pattern, fixed pelvic pain, purple tongue
- Phlegm-Damp (PCOS pattern): irregular/absent periods, obesity, hirsutism, slippery pulse, greasy coat
- Liver Qi stagnation: irregular cycles linked to stress, emotional disruption, wiry pulse

**Integration:** Acupuncture during IVF cycles — evidence is contested but some meta-analyses show modest improvement in live birth rates (particularly around embryo transfer). Herbal formulas: sequence by cycle phase (follicular: Kidney Yin tonification; luteal: Kidney Yang tonification) mirrors biomedical understanding of folliculogenesis and implantation support. Critical: communicate with fertility specialist about all herbs — some may interfere with medications.

### Depression

**Biomedical:** Complex neurobiological — monoamine hypothesis (serotonin, dopamine, norepinephrine), neuroinflammation hypothesis, HPA axis dysregulation, neuroplasticity (BDNF), gut-brain axis

**TCM lens:**
- Liver Qi stagnation: most common — frustrated, constrained, hypochondriac pressure, wiry pulse, may have depressed mood with significant irritability
- Heart and Spleen Blood deficiency: exhausted depression, poor memory, palpitations, insomnia, pale tongue
- Phlegm misting Mind: foggy, heavy depression, unclear thinking, greasy coat
- Kidney Yang deficiency: profound withdrawal, cold, lack of will, deep slow pulse — "empty will"
- Liver Qi stagnation transforming Fire: agitated depression, irritability, sleep disruption, red tongue

**Evidence:** Meta-analyses of acupuncture for depression show effectiveness comparable to antidepressants in mild-moderate depression with fewer side effects. Formulas: Xiao Yao San (Liver Qi stagnation type) has some clinical trial evidence. Gui Pi Tang (Heart-Spleen type) has traditional use evidence. Critical safety: herb-drug interactions with antidepressants — discuss all concurrent medications.

### Type 2 Diabetes

**Biomedical:** Insulin resistance → relative insulin deficiency → chronic hyperglycaemia → vascular, neural, renal complications. Pathways: AMPK downregulation, mTOR dysregulation, mitochondrial dysfunction, chronic low-grade inflammation, gut dysbiosis.

**TCM lens (multiple patterns — critical to differentiate):**
- Kidney Yin deficiency: Classic Xiao Ke (wasting-thirst) presentation — thirst, frequent urination, weight loss, fatigue, red tongue no coat
- Spleen Qi deficiency with Damp-Heat: Overweight/obese type — fatigue, bloating, cravings, greasy coat, heavy feeling (more common in modern T2DM)
- Liver Qi stagnation: Stress-related metabolic syndrome — emotional eating, hypochondriac discomfort, wiry pulse
- Qi and Yin deficiency (most common established T2DM): Fatigue + dryness + weakness

**Integration:** TCM patterns in T2DM correlate with distinct metabolic subtypes. Berberine (Huang Lian) has Level A evidence for glycaemic improvement comparable to metformin in some trials. Yu Nu Jian (Stomach Fire type) and Liu Wei Di Huang Wan (Kidney Yin type) have supporting clinical trial data. Biomedical: do NOT adjust hypoglycaemic medication without physician involvement — hypoglycaemia risk.

### Autoimmune Disease

**Biomedical:** Loss of immune tolerance → self-directed inflammatory response. Multiple subtypes (Th1, Th2, Th17 dominant patterns); gut dysbiosis and leaky gut as triggers; genetic predisposition; environmental triggers.

**TCM lens:**
- No direct TCM equivalent — but patterns consistently emerge:
- Damp-Heat (most inflammatory autoimmune — rheumatoid arthritis, lupus flares, inflammatory bowel disease): Joint swelling/heat, red inflamed tissues, rapid pulse, yellow greasy coat
- Yin deficiency with Empty Heat (Sjögren's, chronic lupus, thyroid autoimmunity): Dry symptoms, afternoon fever, thin rapid pulse, red tongue no coat
- Qi and Blood deficiency (post-flare, chronic fatigue phases): Exhaustion, pallor, weak pulse
- Blood stasis (chronic autoimmune with damage): Fixed pain, purple tongue, choppy pulse

**Integration:** TCM can reduce inflammatory burden and side effects of immunosuppressants; should NOT replace disease-modifying therapy in serious autoimmune disease. Herbs that strongly activate immune response (Huang Qi in large doses) may theoretically worsen autoimmune — use cautiously and pattern-specifically.

### Cognitive Decline and Dementia

**Biomedical:** Multiple pathways — amyloid/tau (Alzheimer's), vascular (vascular dementia), Lewy body, frontotemporal. Shared mechanisms: neuroinflammation, mitochondrial failure, insulin resistance of the brain, synaptic loss.

**TCM lens:**
- Kidney Jing deficiency (primary — the constitution that predisposes): Premature cognitive decline, poor long-term memory, early greying, weak lower back — the root
- Phlegm misting the Mind: Vascular/mixed dementia more common — heavy, cloudy cognition, greasy coat, slippery pulse — the branch accumulation
- Blood stasis obstructing the Brain: Post-stroke cognitive impairment; fixed loss of function; purple tongue
- Heart-Kidney not communicating: Poor working memory + sleep disruption + palpitations — Shen not anchored by Kidney Yin
- Qi and Blood deficiency: Late-stage deficiency pattern — severe atrophy, exhaustion

**Integration:** Rou Cong Rong (Cistanche) has clinical trial data for Alzheimer's mild-moderate cognitive improvement. Tian Ma (gastrodin) neuroprotective evidence. Acupuncture for vascular cognitive impairment: several positive RCTs. TCM best used as prevention and adjunct — not curative once significant neuronal loss has occurred. Longevity protocols (see longevity-protocols.md) are the primary TCM cognitive preservation strategy.

## 5. FUNCTIONAL MEDICINE INTEGRATION

Functional medicine and TCM share a systems-thinking, root-cause orientation. Key integration points:

**Testing that informs TCM pattern selection:**
- Complete blood count → Blood deficiency severity
- Thyroid panel (TSH, T3, T4, rT3) → Kidney Yang deficiency correlate
- Sex hormones (oestrogen, progesterone, testosterone, DHEA, LH, FSH) → Kidney Jing/Yin/Yang axis
- Cortisol curve (4-point salivary) → HPA axis pattern → Kidney Yin/Yang deficiency temporal pattern
- HbA1c and fasting glucose → Spleen Qi/Yang deficiency; Damp accumulation
- Inflammatory markers (CRP, IL-6, ESR) → Heat vs Cold; Stasis vs Deficiency
- Gut microbiome testing → Spleen/Stomach patterns with specific dysbiosis data
- Genetic SNPs (MTHFR, COMT, CYP variants) → constitutional Jing-level information

**Integration protocol:**
1. Biomedical testing establishes objective baseline
2. TCM pattern differentiation identifies pattern (may be multiple)
3. Treatment plan combines: herbs (informed by both pattern and biomedical findings), acupuncture (pattern-based), dietary guidance (TCM thermal + functional nutrition), lifestyle/Qigong prescription
4. Follow-up: both biomarker change AND TCM symptom/tongue/pulse progression

## 6. PRACTICAL INTEGRATION PROTOCOLS

### Patient Communication
- Always ask about ALL medications, supplements, and treatments
- Inform patients when herbs may interact with medications
- Recommend informing GP/specialist about TCM treatment
- Do not advise stopping medications without consulting prescribing physician
- For cancer patients: coordinate with oncologist — especially around timing of chemotherapy

### When to Refer to Western Medicine
- Unexplained weight loss > 5kg
- Persistent fever without obvious cause
- Blood in stool, urine, or sputum (undiagnosed)
- Severe or new neurological symptoms
- Chest pain (cardiac)
- Acute abdomen
- Stroke symptoms
- Any condition where delayed diagnosis could be life-threatening

### When TCM Adds Greatest Value
- Chronic conditions (>3 months) where biomedicine has limited options
- Functional disorders (no clear biomedical pathology)
- Side effect management (chemotherapy, radiation, medication)
- Constitutional optimisation (longevity, preventive medicine)
- Reproductive medicine (fertility, menopause)
- Stress-related psychosomatic conditions
- Post-illness recovery (post-COVID, post-infection fatigue)

### Documentation Standards in Integrative Practice
- Record TCM diagnosis (pattern name) AND biomedical diagnosis
- Note tongue and pulse findings at each session
- Document formula/point prescription with reasoning
- Track both subjective (symptom changes) and objective (biomarker) outcomes
- Record all concurrent medications and supplements
- Note any herb-drug interaction concerns discussed with patient
