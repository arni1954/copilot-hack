## 1. THE 12 PRIMARY CHANNELS

### Lung Channel (Tai Yin — Hand)
**Pathway:** Starts in Middle Jiao → descends to Large Intestine → ascends through diaphragm → enters Lung → throat → emerges at LU-1 → runs down arm → ends at LU-11 (thumb nail)
**External branch:** Axilla → along anterior arm → wrist → thenar eminence → LU-11
**Clinical scope:** Respiratory disease; skin conditions (Lung governs skin and body hair); immunity/Wei Qi; grief; voice; large intestine disease

**Key points:** LU-1 (Front Mu), LU-7 (Luo-Connecting; Command for head/neck), LU-9 (Source; tonify Lung), LU-5 (He-Sea; clear Lung Heat/phlegm), LU-11 (Jing-Well; acute throat)

### Large Intestine Channel (Yang Ming — Hand)
**Pathway:** LI-1 (index finger) → dorsum of hand → wrist → forearm → elbow → upper arm → shoulder (LI-15) → crosses behind neck → DU-14 → ST-12 → Lung → descends to Large Intestine → face branch: ST-12 → neck → jaw → LI-20 (beside nose)
**Clinical scope:** Face, head, throat, teeth, nose conditions; heat patterns; defensive Qi; Large Intestine

**Key points:** LI-4 (Source; pain/face — contraindicated pregnancy), LI-11 (He-Sea; clear Heat; immune), LI-20 (local face/nose)

### Stomach Channel (Yang Ming — Foot)
**Pathway:** Starts ST-1 (below eye) → face → head branch → neck → chest → descends along anterior body → abdomen → inguinal region → thigh → knee → lower leg → dorsum of foot → ST-45 (2nd toe)
**Clinical scope:** Digestive disorders; face; mental illness (Yang Ming excess); hunger/thirst; blood disorders; fever

**Key points:** ST-25 (Front Mu of LI), ST-36 (He-Sea; master tonic point — Qi and Blood; immune), ST-40 (Luo; resolve Phlegm), ST-44 (Spring; clear Heat in channel/Stomach), ST-45 (clear mental agitation)

### Spleen Channel (Tai Yin — Foot)
**Pathway:** SP-1 (big toe medial) → medial foot → ankle → medial lower leg → medial knee → medial thigh → abdomen (SP-13) → Spleen → Stomach → through diaphragm → esophagus → tongue root
**Clinical scope:** Digestive/metabolic; Blood control; muscle/flesh; worry; gynaecology; fluid metabolism

**Key points:** SP-1 (Jing-Well; stop bleeding — moxa), SP-3 (Source; tonify Spleen), SP-6 (Meeting of 3 Yin — Spleen/Liver/Kidney; most versatile point in gynecology and Yin tonification), SP-9 (He-Sea; resolve Damp), SP-10 (cool Blood; invigorate Blood — Blood disorders, skin)

### Heart Channel (Shao Yin — Hand)
**Pathway:** Heart → descends to Small Intestine; ascending branch: Heart → Lung → axilla → arm → elbow → wrist → HT-7 → palm → HT-9 (little finger)
**Clinical scope:** Cardiac conditions; mental/emotional (Shen); sleep; speech; sweating; tongue

**Key points:** HT-3 (He-Sea; calm Shen; Heart Blood), HT-6 (Xi-Cleft; night sweats; Heart Yin deficiency), HT-7 (Source; tonify Heart; calm Shen — insomnia, anxiety), HT-8 (Spring; clear Heart Fire), HT-9 (Jing-Well; resuscitation; clear Heat)

### Small Intestine Channel (Tai Yang — Hand)
**Pathway:** SI-1 (little finger) → dorsum of hand → wrist → posterior forearm → elbow → upper arm → shoulder → DU-14 → clavicle → Heart → descends to SI; face branch: neck → jaw → lateral face → SI-19 (pre-auricular)
**Clinical scope:** Ear, face, neck, shoulder conditions; separating pure/impure (digestive/fluid metabolism); Heart-Small Intestine axis (mental clarity)

**Key points:** SI-3 (Wood/He; opens Du Mai; neck/back/posterior head), SI-8 (local elbow), SI-11 (local shoulder/scapula), SI-19 (ear)

### Bladder Channel (Tai Yang — Foot)
**Pathway:** BL-1 (inner canthus) → forehead → vertex (DU-20 branch) → posterior head → neck → divides: Inner branch → parallel to spine (1.5 cun) → sacrum → back of thigh → popliteal fossa; Outer branch → (3 cun from spine) → buttock → back of thigh → joins inner at BL-40 → lower leg → BL-67 (little toe)
**Clinical scope:** Posterior head, neck, back, spine; eyes; entire nervous system (Bladder Back Shu points); kidney/bladder; fear

**Key points:** BL-2 (eye), BL-10 (neck/head), BL-11 to BL-54 (Back Shu points for all organs — see below), BL-40 (He-Sea; back pain — "command point" for back), BL-60 (river point; cervical pain/headache), BL-67 (moxibustion — turn malpositioned fetus)

### Kidney Channel (Shao Yin — Foot)
**Pathway:** KD-1 (sole of foot) → heel → medial ankle → medial lower leg → medial knee → medial thigh → pubic region → abdomen (0.5 cun from midline) → Kidney → Bladder; internal branch: Kidney → Liver → Lung → Heart → KD-27
**Clinical scope:** Reproductive; bone/marrow/brain; Jing; ears; lower back; fear/willpower; ancestral constitutional health

**Key points:** KD-1 (Jing-Well; emergency; ground Shen — insomnia, emergency), KD-3 (Source; tonify Kidney Yin and Yang), KD-6 (opens Yin Qiao Mai; moisten Yin; insomnia — waking), KD-7 (Metal/River; tonify Kidney Yang; sweating regulation), KD-10 (He-Sea; tonify Kidney Yin)

### Pericardium Channel (Jue Yin — Hand)
**Pathway:** PC-1 (nipple) → chest → Pericardium → diaphragm → Triple Warmer; arm branch: PC-1 → axilla → PC-3 → forearm → wrist → palm → PC-9 (middle finger)
**Clinical scope:** Heart protector — cardiac and emotional; mental illness (partner to Heart); nausea/vomiting (passes through diaphragm/Stomach); blood and Shen

**Key points:** PC-3 (He-Sea; clear Heart Heat), PC-5 (Wood; resolve Phlegm misting Shen), PC-6 (Luo; opens Yin Wei Mai; nausea; chest pain; Shen), PC-8 (Spring; clear Heart Fire), PC-9 (Jing-Well; resuscitation)

### Triple Warmer / San Jiao Channel (Shao Yang — Hand)
**Pathway:** SJ-1 (ring finger) → dorsum of hand → wrist → posterior forearm → olecranon → upper arm → shoulder → DU-14 → clavicle → Pericardium → San Jiao; face branch: neck → posterior ear → SJ-23
**Clinical scope:** Fluid metabolism regulation; ear; lateral face/neck/shoulder; fever (Shao Yang); immune; opens Dai Mai (GB-41 coupled)

**Key points:** SJ-3 (local hand; opens Yang channels), SJ-5 (Luo; opens Yang Wei Mai; fever/exterior patterns; lateral head/neck), SJ-6 (constipation; hypochondriac pain), SJ-17 (ear)

### Gallbladder Channel (Shao Yang — Foot)
**Pathway:** GB-1 (outer canthus) → temporal head (zigzag) → posterior head → neck → shoulder → DU-14 → descends lateral chest and ribs → hip → lateral thigh and leg → GB-44 (4th toe)
**Clinical scope:** Lateral body; head (lateral); eyes; bitter taste; decision-making; Liver-GB axis; tendons

**Key points:** GB-2 (ear), GB-12 (posterior head), GB-14 (frontal headache), GB-20 (Wind gate — headache; hypertension; eye; Wei Qi), GB-21 (shoulder; labour — contraindicated pregnancy), GB-25 (Front Mu Kidney), GB-29 (hip), GB-34 (He-Sea; influential for tendons/sinews; GB/Liver; lateral knee), GB-39 (influential for marrow; bone disease), GB-40 (Source), GB-41 (Wood; opens Dai Mai), GB-43 (clear Liver/GB Fire)

### Liver Channel (Jue Yin — Foot)
**Pathway:** LV-1 (big toe medial) → dorsum foot → ankle → medial lower leg → knee → medial thigh → genitals → lower abdomen → Stomach → Liver → Gallbladder → diaphragm → hypochondrium → throat → eyes → vertex (DU-20)
**Clinical scope:** Emotions (anger/depression); eyes; genitals; menstruation; tendons; smooth flow of Qi throughout body

**Key points:** LV-1 (Jing-Well; stop genital bleeding — moxa), LV-2 (Spring; clear Liver Fire), LV-3 (Source; spread Liver Qi — the most-used Liver point; combine with LI-4 = Four Gates), LV-8 (He-Sea; tonify Liver Blood and Yin), LV-13 (Front Mu Spleen; influential for Zang organs), LV-14 (Front Mu Liver; spread Liver Qi)

## 2. KEY POINT CATEGORIES

### Five Shu (Transport) Points
Each channel has 5 transport points from distal to proximal: Jing-Well → Ying-Spring → Shu-Stream → Jing-River → He-Sea

| Category | Element | Clinical Use |
|---|---|---|
| Jing-Well | Wood (Yin)/Metal (Yang) | Acute conditions; resuscitation; clear Heat distally |
| Ying-Spring | Fire (Yin)/Water (Yang) | Fever; Heat in channel |
| Shu-Stream | Earth (Yin)/Wood (Yang) | Pain/heaviness in joints; intermittent conditions; tonification on Yin channels |
| Jing-River | Metal (Yin)/Fire (Yang) | Cough/dyspnoea; voice; throat |
| He-Sea | Water (Yin)/Earth (Yang) | Disorders of Yang organs (Fu); running counter-current Qi |

### Source (Yuan) Points
- One per channel; tonify the Source Qi of that organ
- Most important tonification points for each organ's Qi deficiency

### Luo (Connecting) Points
- Connect paired Yin-Yang channels
- Key for treating excess in one channel with deficiency in its pair
- Also for chronic conditions and psycho-emotional work

### Back Shu and Front Mu Points
- Back Shu: Best for chronic/deficiency conditions of the organ (tonify)
- Front Mu: Better for acute/excess conditions (disperse)
- Shu-Mu combination: Powerful for acute exacerbations of chronic organ disease

### Eight Influential Points (Ba Hui Xue)
| Domain | Point |
|---|---|
| Zang organs | LV-13 |
| Fu organs | REN-12 |
| Qi | REN-17 |
| Blood | BL-17 |
| Tendons/Sinews | GB-34 |
| Vessels/Pulse | LU-9 |
| Bone | GB-39 |
| Marrow | GB-39 (also) |

## 3. ESSENTIAL POINT FUNCTIONS — MASTER REFERENCE

**ST-36 (Zu San Li — Leg Three Miles)**
The most important single point in Chinese medicine for tonification.
- Tonifies Qi and Blood; strengthens Spleen/Stomach; supports Wei Qi; anti-ageing longevity point
- Direct moxa: Classical protocol for longevity (moxa 3-5 cones, lifetime practice)
- Clinical: Post-illness recovery, chronic fatigue, immune deficiency, nutritional deficiency, ALL deficiency states

**SP-6 (San Yin Jiao — Three Yin Intersection)**
Meeting point of Spleen, Liver, Kidney Yin channels — broadest therapeutic range of any single point.
- Tonifies Yin (all three); regulates Blood; calms Shen; treats gynaecological conditions
- **Contraindicated in pregnancy** (strong moving action)
- Clinical: Insomnia, anxiety, menstrual irregularity, hormonal conditions, Yin deficiency patterns, oedema, digestive issues

**LV-3 (Tai Chong — Great Surge)**
The most important point for Liver Qi regulation.
- Spreads Liver Qi; calms Liver Yang; nourishes Liver Blood; clears Liver Fire
- Combined with LI-4 = Four Gates: Opens Qi and Blood movement throughout body; strong dispersing action

**KD-3 (Tai Xi — Great Ravine)**
Most important point for tonifying Kidney (both Yin and Yang).
- Tonifies Kidney Yuan Qi; nourishes Yin; strengthens Yang
- Clinical: Lower back pain (chronic), tinnitus, hormonal decline, reproductive issues, fatigue

**PC-6 (Nei Guan — Inner Pass)**
- Opens chest; calms Shen; stops nausea/vomiting; opens Yin Wei Mai
- Among the most versatile points in clinical practice
- Clinical: Nausea (including chemotherapy-induced — strong evidence), chest pain, palpitations, anxiety, insomnia

**DU-20 (Bai Hui — Hundred Meetings)**
- Lifts Yang Qi; calms Shen; clears the mind; benefits the brain
- Moxa: Raises Spleen Qi (prolapse); tonifies Yang
- Needled: Calms Shen; descends Liver Yang
- Clinical: Prolapse, depression (lifting Yang), vertex headache, insomnia, cognitive issues

**REN-4 (Guan Yuan — Gate of Yuan Qi)**
- Fortifies Yuan Qi; tonifies Kidney; nourishes Blood; warms Lower Jiao
- Major moxa point for deep Yang tonification and longevity
- Clinical: Chronic fatigue, reproductive deficiency, urinary frequency, cold lower abdomen

**REN-6 (Qi Hai — Sea of Qi)**
- Tonifies Yuan Qi; strengthens Kidney; moves Qi in Lower Jiao
- Moxa: Warms and tonifies Qi in the entire lower abdomen

**BL-40 (Wei Zhong — Commanding Middle)**
- He-Sea of Bladder; "Command point for the back"
- Clears Heat from Blood; benefits lumbar spine
- Classical: "For lumbar and back diseases, seek Wei Zhong first"

## 4. NEEDLING STRATEGY PRINCIPLES

### Root vs Branch Treatment
- **Branch (Biao):** Acute symptomatic relief — use local and empirical points; provides immediate response
- **Root (Ben):** Constitutional treatment — uses organ Source and Back Shu points; creates lasting change
- Clinical strategy: In acute disease treat Branch first; in chronic disease treat Root primarily (may add Branch symptomatic points)

### Reinforcing vs Reducing Technique (Bu Xie 補瀉)
**Tonification (Bu):**
- Insert needle with the channel flow direction
- Rotate clockwise (for Yin channels) — or gently, slowly
- Retain with patient relaxed; remove quickly, close hole
- Moxa: Always tonifying

**Reduction (Xie):**
- Insert needle against channel flow direction
- Vigorous manipulation; even rotation or counter-clockwise
- Remove slowly; do not close hole

*Note: In modern practice, needle sensation (De Qi 得氣) is the primary therapeutic goal — the aching, heavy, distending sensation confirming arrival of Qi.*

### Point Combination Frameworks

**Distal-Local Combination:**
- Distal point on relevant channel + local point at site of pathology
- Example: Headache (Bladder channel): BL-60 (distal) + BL-10 (local)

**Front-Back Combination (Shu-Mu):**
- Back Shu + Front Mu of same organ
- Example: Liver: BL-18 + LV-14 — powerful for acute Liver conditions

**Upper-Lower Combination:**
- Pairs point above waist with corresponding point below
- Example: PC-6 (upper) + SP-4 (lower) — opens Yin Wei Mai

**Left-Right (Contralateral):**
- Classical: Treat opposite side for pain syndromes ("cross-needling")
- Strong stimulation of healthy side activates reflex healing in injured side

### Common Clinical Protocols

**Insomnia Protocol:**
HT-7, SP-6, KD-3, BL-15 (Back Shu Heart), BL-23 (Back Shu Kidney), DU-20, Anmian (extra)
Modifications: Night sweats add HT-6, KD-6; Phlegm add ST-40, PC-5; Liver stagnation add LV-3

**Anxiety/Depression Protocol:**
DU-20, PC-6, HT-7, LV-3, SP-6, REN-14, REN-17
Modifications: Depression (Qi deficiency type) add ST-36; Phlegm add ST-40; Stasis add SP-10

**Menstrual Irregularity Protocol:**
SP-6, SP-10, LV-3, SP-8 (Xi-Cleft for dysmenorrhoea), REN-4, ST-25
Modifications: Blood stasis add BL-17, SP-10; Deficiency add ST-36, BL-20

**Chronic Fatigue Protocol:**
ST-36 (moxa), SP-6, REN-4 (moxa), DU-20, BL-20, BL-23, LU-9
Core: Tonify Spleen (ST-36, BL-20), Kidney (BL-23, REN-4), Lung (LU-9)

**Back Pain Protocol:**
BL-40 (command point), BL-23 (Back Shu Kidney), DU-4, GV-3, local Bladder channel points (BL-25-BL-32 depending on level)
Modifications: Kidney Yang deficiency — moxa BL-23, DU-4; Qi stagnation — add BL-17; Sciatica — add GB-30, GB-34, BL-36

## 5. DIVERGENT CHANNELS (JING BIE 經別) AND SINEW CHANNELS (JING JIN 經筋)

### Divergent Channels (Jing Bie 經別)

The 12 Divergent channels branch off the primary channels, enter deep into the body cavities, and connect to their respective organs before resurfacing at the head and neck. They extend channel reach into areas the primary channels do not directly access.

**Clinical significance:**
- Treat organ pathology accessed via channel pathways (e.g., Heart issues via Small Intestine divergent)
- Treat areas not directly on the primary channel pathway
- Address Yin-Yang organ pairs at a deeper level
- Particularly useful when primary channel treatment is insufficient

**Key Divergent channel principles:**
- They all eventually resurface at the neck/head — explains why many head/neck points treat deep organ conditions
- The Yin divergents unite with their Yang pair before resurfacing — this is why Yang channel points can treat Yin organ pathology
- The six "Confluences" (He 合) are the meeting points of Yin-Yang paired divergents

**Primary clinical applications:**
- Divergent channels treat regions between the primary channel pathway and the organ (e.g., chest, axilla, groin)
- Heart Divergent: Throat and face — explains HT-5 and HT-7 effect on tongue and speech disorders
- Bladder Divergent: Rectum and anus — explains BL channel points treating haemorrhoids and rectal prolapse
- Stomach Divergent: Heart — explains ST channel points (ST-36, ST-40) influence on Shen and cardiac function
- Use divergents when: Primary channel treatment not working; pathology in channel "gap" areas; complex multi-system disease

### Sinew Channels (Jing Jin 經筋)

The 12 Sinew channels follow the primary channel pathways but are entirely superficial — they run through muscle and connective tissue without entering the body cavities or connecting to organs. They govern musculoskeletal function.

**Clinical significance:**
- The primary framework for musculoskeletal and pain disorders in TCM
- Treat muscle, tendon, ligament, fascial pain along channel pathways
- Do not treat organ pathology — use primary channels for that
- Affected by external pathogens (especially Wind-Cold-Damp) causing Bi syndrome (painful obstruction)

**Bi Syndrome (Bi Zheng 痺證) — the primary sinew channel disorder:**
- Wind Bi (Xing Bi): Moving/wandering pain — Wind dominant
- Cold Bi (Tong Bi): Severe fixed pain, worse cold — Cold dominant
- Damp Bi (Zhuo Bi): Fixed heavy pain, swollen joints — Damp dominant
- Heat Bi (Re Bi): Hot, red, swollen joints — Damp-Heat; acute inflammatory arthritis
- Bone Bi (Gu Bi): Chronic, deforming — long-term invasion deep to bone

**Sinew channel treatment principles:**
- Treat locally along the affected sinew channel
- Use Ah Shi points (most tender/reactive points) — these are often more important than named points
- Moxa and cupping are primary for Cold-Damp Bi
- Bleeding and cupping for Heat Bi (remove Heat from sinews)
- Electro-acupuncture effective for chronic sinew channel pathology

**Specific sinew channel clinical applications:**
- Bladder Sinew Channel: All posterior back and neck pain; occipital headache; hamstring/calf
- Gallbladder Sinew Channel: Lateral body; IT band; lateral knee; temporal headache
- Stomach Sinew Channel: Anterior thigh; shin; facial tension; involuntary muscle contractions (facial spasm)
- Large Intestine Sinew Channel: Lateral elbow (tennis elbow); shoulder; neck lateral
- Small Intestine Sinew Channel: Posterior shoulder; infraspinatus; medial elbow
- Liver Sinew Channel: Medial thigh/groin; genitals; inner knee

## 6. SAFETY AND CONTRAINDICATIONS

**Absolute contraindications:**
- Pregnancy: SP-6, LI-4, GB-21, BL-67, SP-8, LV-3 (large doses), any strong moving points in Lower Jiao
- Moxa near mucous membranes, eyes, or compromised skin
- Needle directly into active infection site

**Relative contraindications:**
- Anticoagulants: Use fewer needles; avoid deep needling
- Severe immunosuppression: Strict sterile technique; avoid immune-depleting points
- Active haemorrhage: Avoid moving-Blood points
- Extreme deficiency: Gentle technique; fewer points; shorter retention
- Cancer: Avoid direct needling over tumour sites; Back Shu points in metastatic spine disease

**Needle depth and direction guidelines:**
- Thoracic Back Shu points: Oblique needling (15-30°) toward spine; maximum 0.5 cun — pneumothorax risk
- DU-16 (Feng Fu): Avoid perpendicular deep needling — brainstem proximity
- Kidney area: Avoid deep perpendicular needling
- Always aspirate before injecting in wet needling/pharmacopuncture
