## 1. EIGHT PRINCIPLES — FULL CLINICAL DETAIL

### Yin/Yang
The master axis containing all others.

**Yin patterns:** Interior, Cold, Deficiency tendency — slow onset, chronic, quiet presentation, cold body, pale tongue, deep slow pulse
**Yang patterns:** Exterior, Hot, Excess tendency — rapid onset, acute, agitated presentation, warm body, red tongue, rapid pulse

**Mixed Yin-Yang:** Extremely common clinically. True cold (Yin) with false heat (Yang) appearance = Yang collapse. True heat (Yang) with cold extremities = interior Heat with Qi constraint preventing distribution.

### Interior/Exterior
**Exterior (Biao 表):** Pathogen in Wei (Defensive) layer — chills, fever, neck stiffness, body aches, floating pulse. Recent onset. Treat first if concurrent with interior pattern (unless interior is severe).

**Interior (Li 裏):** Pathogen or dysfunction within Zang-Fu organs. Majority of clinical presentations.

**Half-interior Half-exterior (Shao Yang):** Classic Shang Han stage — alternating chills/fever, bitter taste, nausea, hypochondriac fullness, wiry pulse. Formula: Xiao Chai Hu Tang.

### Cold/Hot
**Cold patterns:**
- Excess Cold: Pathogen invasion — acute sharp cold pain, no thirst, white coat, tight pulse
- Deficiency Cold: Insufficient Yang — chronic dull cold sensations, cold limbs, frequent clear urination, loose stools, pale tongue, deep weak pulse
- False Heat with True Cold: Red face but cold body, restless but wanting warmth, weak deep pulse — a critical distinction; warming treatment required

**Hot patterns:**
- Excess Heat: Red face, thirst, constipation, dark urine, yellow coat, rapid forceful pulse
- Deficiency Heat (Empty Heat): Night sweats, afternoon fever sensation, five-centre heat (palms/soles/sternum), red tongue no coat, thin rapid pulse — Yin deficiency generating Heat
- False Cold with True Heat: Cold extremities with heat in trunk, hot urine, constipation, deep but rapid pulse — interior Heat with peripheral Qi stagnation

### Excess/Deficiency
**Excess (Shi 實):** Strong constitution, recent onset, strong pulse, thick coat. Pathogen presence (Cold, Heat, Dampness, Phlegm, Blood stasis, Qi stagnation).

**Deficiency (Xu 虛):** Weak constitution, chronic, weak pulse, thin or absent coat. Insufficient Qi, Blood, Yin, or Yang.

**Mixed Excess/Deficiency:** Most chronic disease. Underlying deficiency with secondary pathogenic accumulation (e.g., Spleen Qi deficiency → Damp accumulation → Phlegm). Treat both; sequence depends on presentation.

## 2. ZANG-FU PATTERNS — COMPLETE CLINICAL LIBRARY

### HEART (Xin 心)

**Heart Qi Deficiency (Xin Qi Xu)**
- Symptoms: Palpitations (worse exertion), shortness of breath (exertion), fatigue, spontaneous sweating, pale complexion
- Tongue: Pale, slightly swollen
- Pulse: Empty, weak (especially Cun left)
- Formula: Gui Pi Tang (if concurrent Spleen Qi deficiency); Yang Xin Tang
- Points: HT-7, PC-6, REN-14, REN-6, ST-36, SP-6

**Heart Yang Deficiency (Xin Yang Xu)**
- As above + cold sensations, cold limbs, purple lips, oedema
- Severe: Heart Yang collapse (syncope, cyanosis, profuse cold sweat) — emergency
- Formula: Zhi Gan Cao Tang; Gui Zhi Gan Cao Tang
- Points: Above + DU-14, moxa REN-4, REN-8

**Heart Blood Deficiency (Xin Xue Xu)**
- Palpitations, insomnia (difficulty falling asleep), dream-disturbed sleep, poor memory, anxiety, dizziness, pallor, pale lips
- Tongue: Pale, thin
- Pulse: Thready, choppy
- Formula: Gui Pi Tang; Suan Zao Ren Tang (if insomnia dominant)
- Points: HT-7, SP-6, BL-15, BL-17, BL-20, REN-14

**Heart Yin Deficiency (Xin Yin Xu)**
- As Heart Blood deficiency + night sweats, afternoon fever sensation, five-centre heat, dry mouth/throat, restlessness
- Tongue: Red, no coat or peeled coat, possibly cracked
- Pulse: Thready, rapid
- Formula: Tian Wang Bu Xin Dan; Bai He Di Huang Tang (if Lung Yin also involved)
- Points: HT-6, HT-7, KD-6, SP-6, REN-14, BL-15

**Heart Fire (Xin Huo)**
- Agitation, restlessness, insomnia, palpitations, thirst, mouth/tongue ulcers, dark scanty urine, possibly bitter taste
- Tongue: Red (especially tip), yellow coat
- Pulse: Rapid, full (especially Cun left)
- Formula: Dao Chi San; Huang Lian Jie Du Tang (if severe)
- Points: HT-8, HT-9, PC-8, BL-15, SP-6, KD-6 (to anchor descending Fire)

**Phlegm-Fire Harassing Heart (Tan Huo Rao Xin)**
- Insomnia, agitation, incoherent speech, possible mania, laughing/crying inappropriately, palpitations, bitter taste, dizziness, nausea
- Tongue: Red, greasy yellow coat
- Pulse: Rapid, slippery, wiry
- Formula: Wen Dan Tang + Huang Lian; An Gong Niu Huang Wan (severe)
- Points: HT-7, PC-5, ST-40, DU-20, LV-2, GB-34

**Phlegm Misting Heart (Tan Mi Xin Qiao)**
- Mental confusion, clouded thinking, possible unconsciousness, incoherent speech, rattling phlegm sounds
- Tongue: White thick greasy coat
- Pulse: Slippery, slow or wiry
- Formula: Di Tan Tang; Su He Xiang Wan (unconsciousness, cold type)
- Points: PC-9, DU-26, ST-40, REN-12, BL-15

### LIVER (Gan 肝)

**Liver Qi Stagnation (Gan Qi Yu Jie)**
- Most common pattern in modern clinical practice
- Symptoms: Distending/moving pain (hypochondria, chest, epigastrium, lower abdomen), emotional: depression, irritability, frustration, mood swings; sighing; throat fullness (Plum Stone Qi); irregular menstruation; breast distension pre-menstrually; bowel irregularity
- Tongue: Often normal, or slightly dusky at edges; slight purple tinge in severe cases
- Pulse: Wiry (especially Guan left)
- Formula: Chai Hu Shu Gan San; Xiao Yao San (if concurrent Spleen Qi deficiency and Blood deficiency)
- Points: LV-3, LV-14, PC-6, GB-34, REN-17, SJ-6, SP-6

**Liver Fire Rising (Gan Huo Shang Yan)**
- Stagnation generating Heat; ascends to head
- Symptoms: Severe headache (splitting, temporal or vertex), red eyes (bloodshot), tinnitus (loud), sudden deafness, bitter taste, irritability/anger, dry mouth, constipation, dark urine
- Tongue: Red, especially edges; yellow dry coat
- Pulse: Wiry, rapid
- Formula: Long Dan Xie Gan Tang; Tian Ma Gou Teng Yin (if hypertension component)
- Points: LV-2, LV-3, GB-43, GB-44, LI-4, DU-20, GB-20

**Liver Yang Rising (Gan Yang Shang Kang)**
- Underlying Liver Yin (or Kidney Yin) deficiency → Yang not anchored → rises
- Symptoms: Dizziness (worse stress/tiredness), tinnitus, headache (dull), irritability, dry eyes, insomnia, lower backache, afternoon heat sensation
- Tongue: Red, dry, possibly no coat at root (Kidney Yin depleted)
- Pulse: Wiry, thready (excess above, deficiency below)
- Formula: Tian Ma Gou Teng Yin; Qi Ju Di Huang Wan (if Kidney/Liver Yin deficiency dominant)
- Points: LV-3, KD-3, KD-6, SP-6, GB-20, DU-20, BL-18, BL-23

**Liver Wind (Gan Feng)**
Three subtypes:
1. **Extreme Heat generating Wind:** Convulsions, high fever, delirium — paediatric febrile convulsions, acute stroke
2. **Liver Yang transforming to Wind:** Tremors, numbness, dizziness, possible stroke — chronic hypertension progression
3. **Liver Blood deficiency generating Wind:** Muscle spasms, cramps, numbness, twitching — deficiency context

- Formula: Tian Ma Gou Teng Yin; Ling Jiao Gou Teng Tang (heat generating wind)
- Points: LV-3, DU-16, DU-20, GB-20, BL-18; LI-4 + LV-3 (Four Gates) for Wind

**Liver Blood Deficiency (Gan Xue Xu)**
- Symptoms: Pale complexion, pale nails, dry hair, blurred vision/floaters, dry eyes, muscle cramps/numbness, insomnia (difficulty staying asleep), scanty/pale menstruation or amenorrhoea, dizziness
- Tongue: Pale, thin
- Pulse: Thready, choppy (especially Guan left)
- Formula: Si Wu Tang (foundation); Gui Pi Tang (concurrent Spleen); Suan Zao Ren Tang (insomnia dominant)
- Points: LV-8, SP-6, BL-18, BL-17, ST-36

**Liver Yin Deficiency (Gan Yin Xu)**
- As Blood deficiency + Deficiency Heat signs: night sweats, afternoon fever sensation, five-centre heat, dry mouth
- Often concurrent with Kidney Yin deficiency (Liver-Kidney Yin deficiency — common menopausal pattern)
- Formula: Yi Guan Jian; Qi Ju Di Huang Wan
- Points: LV-8, KD-3, SP-6, HT-6, BL-18, BL-23

**Liver Channel Cold (Gan Han)**
- Cold pathogen in Liver channel (Jue Yin)
- Symptoms: Cold pain in lower abdomen/genitals, testicular retraction/pain, hernia pain — worse cold, better warmth; pale tongue; deep wiry or tight pulse
- Formula: Nuan Gan Jian; Wu Zhu Yu Tang (if vomiting concurrent)
- Points: LV-1, LV-3, SP-6, REN-4, moxa ST-29, moxa DU-4

### SPLEEN (Pi 脾)

**Spleen Qi Deficiency (Pi Qi Xu)**
- Foundational pattern — extremely common; often the root cause of secondary pathologies
- Symptoms: Fatigue (worse after eating), poor appetite, abdominal distension (post-meal), loose stools, pallor, muscle weakness, easy bruising, prolapse tendency, bloating
- Tongue: Pale, swollen, teeth-marks, thin white coat
- Pulse: Weak, soft (especially Guan right)
- Formula: Si Jun Zi Tang (base); Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang (if prolapse/lifting sensation); Xiang Sha Liu Jun Zi Tang (if Qi stagnation and Damp concurrent)
- Points: SP-3, SP-6, ST-36, REN-12, BL-20, BL-21, DU-20 (prolapse)

**Spleen Yang Deficiency (Pi Yang Xu)**
- As Qi deficiency + cold signs: cold limbs, abdominal cold pain (better warmth/pressure), watery diarrhoea (possibly with undigested food), oedema
- Tongue: Pale, swollen, wet, white coat
- Pulse: Deep, slow, weak
- Formula: Li Zhong Wan; Fu Zi Li Zhong Wan (severe cold); Zhen Wu Tang (oedema dominant)
- Points: Above + moxa REN-8, moxa ST-25, BL-23

**Spleen Qi Sinking (Pi Qi Xia Xian)**
- Spleen Qi deficiency failing to hold up organs
- Symptoms: Sensation of internal organs pulling down, prolapsed organs (uterine, rectal, stomach, kidney), haemorrhoids, chronic diarrhoea, chronic fatigue, faint voice
- Formula: Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang (primary formula for this pattern)
- Points: DU-20 (moxa — lifts Yang), SP-6, ST-36, REN-12, BL-20

**Spleen Not Governing Blood (Pi Bu Tong Xue)**
- Spleen Qi failing to hold Blood in vessels
- Symptoms: Chronic bleeding (easy bruising, petechiae, heavy menstruation, chronic mild haematuria, nose bleeds), in context of Spleen Qi deficiency presentation
- Formula: Gui Pi Tang (primary); Huang Tu Tang (digestive bleeding with cold)
- Points: SP-1 (moxa — stops bleeding), SP-6, ST-36, BL-20, REN-12

**Cold-Damp Obstructing Spleen (Han Shi Kun Pi)**
- Externally contracted Damp-Cold suppressing Spleen
- Symptoms: Heavy sensation body/head, abdominal fullness/distension, nausea, no appetite, no thirst or desire for warm drinks only, loose stools, white thick greasy coat
- Formula: Huo Xiang Zheng Qi San; Ping Wei San (if less acute)
- Points: SP-9, ST-25, REN-12, PC-6, SJ-6, ST-40

**Damp-Heat in Spleen (Pi Jing Shi Re)**
- Heat + Damp in Spleen/Stomach/Large Intestine
- Symptoms: Abdominal pain, diarrhoea (smelly, possibly bloody/mucousy), burning anus, scanty dark urine, nausea, bitter taste, body heaviness, fever
- Tongue: Red, thick greasy yellow coat
- Pulse: Slippery, rapid
- Formula: Ge Gen Huang Qin Huang Lian Tang; Yin Chen Hao Tang (if jaundice)
- Points: SP-9, LI-11, ST-25, ST-44, REN-12, BL-20

### LUNG (Fei 肺)

**Lung Qi Deficiency (Fei Qi Xu)**
- Symptoms: Weak cough, low voice, shortness of breath (especially exertion), spontaneous sweating, aversion to cold/wind, frequent respiratory infections, pale complexion, fatigue
- Tongue: Pale, thin white coat
- Pulse: Weak (Cun right), possibly floating empty
- Formula: Bu Fei Tang; Yu Ping Feng San (for recurrent infections, Wei Qi deficiency)
- Points: LU-9, LU-7, ST-36, SP-6, BL-13, REN-17

**Lung Yin Deficiency (Fei Yin Xu)**
- Dry cough (scanty sticky phlegm, possibly blood-tinged), dry throat, hoarseness, afternoon fever sensation, night sweats, five-centre heat, emaciation
- Tongue: Red, dry, no coat (especially anterior/middle)
- Pulse: Thready, rapid
- Formula: Bai He Gu Jin Tang; Sha Shen Mai Dong Tang
- Points: LU-5, LU-9, KD-3, SP-6, BL-13, BL-23, REN-4

**Wind-Cold Invading Lung (Feng Han Fan Fei)**
- Exterior pattern; acute onset
- Symptoms: Chills (dominant over fever), no sweating, nasal congestion (clear discharge), sneezing, occipital headache, body aches, floating tight pulse
- Tongue: Thin white coat
- Formula: Ma Huang Tang (no sweating, severe); Gui Zhi Tang (mild sweating, weaker constitution)
- Points: LU-7, LI-4, BL-12, BL-13, DU-16, GB-20

**Wind-Heat Invading Lung (Feng Re Fan Fei)**
- Exterior Heat pattern; acute
- Symptoms: Fever (dominant over chills), mild sweating, sore throat, yellow nasal discharge, thirst, floating rapid pulse
- Tongue: Thin yellow coat (often still white in very early stage)
- Formula: Yin Qiao San; Sang Ju Yin (milder, cough dominant)
- Points: LU-11, LI-4, LI-11, ST-44, BL-12, BL-13, DU-14

**Phlegm-Cold in Lung (Han Tan Zu Fei)**
- Copious white/clear watery phlegm, cough, wheezing, chest fullness, cold sensations, worse cold weather
- Tongue: Pale, thick white wet coat
- Pulse: Slippery, slow or wiry
- Formula: Xiao Qing Long Tang; Su Zi Jiang Qi Tang (if Kidney Qi failure to grasp concurrent)
- Points: LU-1, LU-7, BL-13, REN-17, REN-22, ST-40, SP-9

**Phlegm-Heat in Lung (Re Tan Zu Fei)**
- Productive cough (yellow/green thick phlegm), chest pain, fever, thirst, constipation, dark urine
- Tongue: Red, thick yellow greasy coat
- Pulse: Slippery, rapid
- Formula: Ma Xing Shi Gan Tang; Ding Chuan Tang (wheezing component); Qing Qi Hua Tan Wan
- Points: LU-5, LU-1, LI-11, ST-40, BL-13, REN-22, DU-14

### KIDNEY (Shen 腎)

*The Kidney is the root of Pre-Heaven constitution. Deficiency patterns are extremely common in chronic disease and ageing.*

**Kidney Yin Deficiency (Shen Yin Xu)**
- Symptoms: Lower backache (dull, chronic), weak knees/legs, tinnitus (low-pitched), slight dizziness, night sweats, five-centre heat, dry mouth (especially night), afternoon fever sensation, scanty dark urine, constipation, insomnia (wakening)
- Tongue: Red, dry, no coat (especially root — kidney area), peeled or cracked
- Pulse: Thready, rapid (especially Chi position)
- Formula: Liu Wei Di Huang Wan (base); Zhi Bai Di Huang Wan (if Heat signs dominant); Zuo Gui Wan (severe deficiency)
- Points: KD-3, KD-6, SP-6, LV-3, BL-23, REN-4

**Kidney Yang Deficiency (Shen Yang Xu)**
- Symptoms: Lower backache (dull, cold — better warmth), cold limbs (especially below waist), frequent pale urination (nocturia common), loose stools (dawn diarrhoea — "cock-crow diarrhoea"), oedema (lower body), impotence, decreased libido, infertility, fatigue (profound)
- Tongue: Pale, swollen, moist/wet, tooth-marks, white coat
- Pulse: Deep, slow, weak (especially Chi)
- Formula: Jin Gui Shen Qi Wan (base); You Gui Wan (severe deficiency); Zhen Wu Tang (oedema dominant)
- Points: BL-23, DU-4 (moxa), REN-4 (moxa), KD-3, ST-36, SP-6

**Kidney Jing Deficiency (Shen Jing Xu)**
- The deepest constitutional deficiency; affects development and ageing processes
- Symptoms: In children: delayed development (bone, teeth, speech, walking); in adults: premature ageing, early greying, hair loss, weak bones (osteoporosis), poor memory (long-term), infertility, low libido, constitutional weakness
- This is the pattern most directly correlated with ageing; longevity medicine aims to preserve Jing
- Formula: He Che Da Zao Wan; Zuo Gui Wan or You Gui Wan depending on Yin/Yang dominance of deficiency
- Points: BL-23, REN-4, DU-4, KD-3, SP-6, ST-36; moxa protocol for severe cases

**Kidney Qi Not Firm (Shen Qi Bu Gu)**
- Kidney Qi insufficient to consolidate/control: leakage patterns
- Symptoms: Urinary incontinence/frequency/nocturia, spermatorrhoea, premature ejaculation, early miscarriage, chronic diarrhoea, weak lower back (worse lifting/exertion), fatigue
- Formula: Jin Suo Gu Jing Wan; Sang Piao Xiao San; Suo Quan Wan (urinary)
- Points: BL-23, KD-3, REN-4, DU-4, SP-6, BL-32 (for urinary control)

**Kidney Failing to Grasp Qi (Shen Bu Na Qi)**
- Kidney role: anchor Qi downward; Lung sends Qi down; Kidney receives it
- Symptoms: Chronic asthma/dyspnoea (worse exertion), inhalation difficult more than exhalation, lower back weak, pale, cold sweating with dyspnoea
- Formula: Ren Shen Hu Tao Tang; Su Zi Jiang Qi Tang; Du Qi Wan
- Points: KD-3, KD-7, LU-7, REN-4, REN-17, BL-23, BL-13; moxa REN-4

### GALLBLADDER (Dan 膽)

*The Gallbladder is the only Fu organ paired with a Zang organ (Liver) that stores a pure (non-turbid) substance — bile. Clinically, Gallbladder patterns often appear alongside Liver patterns and must be differentiated.*

**Gallbladder Damp-Heat (Dan Jing Shi Re)**
- Symptoms: Hypochondriac pain (right-sided), bitter taste, nausea, jaundice (yellow skin/eyes), fever with chills, scanty dark urine, vomiting
- Tongue: Red, thick greasy yellow coat
- Pulse: Wiry, rapid, slippery
- Formula: Long Dan Xie Gan Tang; Yin Chen Hao Tang (jaundice dominant); Da Chai Hu Tang (concurrent Yang Ming stagnation)
- Points: GB-34, GB-24 (Front Mu GB), BL-19 (Back Shu GB), LV-14, SP-9, LI-11

**Gallbladder Qi Deficiency (Dan Qi Xu) — "Timid Gallbladder"**
- Symptoms: Timidity, indecisiveness, easily startled, palpitations, anxiety, insomnia (difficulty falling asleep with anxiety), sighing, dizziness
- This is the pattern behind constitutional shyness and anxiety rooted in Gallbladder insufficiency
- Tongue: Pale or normal
- Pulse: Wiry, thready, slightly weak at Guan
- Formula: Wen Dan Tang (base — warms and tonifies Gallbladder Qi); Chai Hu Long Gu Mu Li Tang (if more pronounced agitation)
- Points: GB-40, BL-19, GB-34, HT-7, SP-6, ST-36

**Phlegm-Heat Disturbing Gallbladder (Tan Re Rao Dan)**
- Symptoms: Restlessness, insomnia (vivid disturbing dreams), bitter taste, nausea, chest oppression, startling easily, dizziness, palpitations
- Often accompanies Heart Shen disturbance — the "Gallbladder agitating the Heart" pattern
- Tongue: Red, greasy yellow coat
- Pulse: Wiry, slippery, rapid
- Formula: Wen Dan Tang + Huang Lian (Huang Lian Wen Dan Tang)
- Points: GB-34, PC-6, HT-7, ST-40, BL-19, GB-40

**Gallbladder Channel Cold (Dan Han)**
- Migraine (classic shao yang headache — temporal), alternating chills and fever, bitter taste, hypochondriac pain
- Formula: Xiao Chai Hu Tang; Wu Zhu Yu Tang (if vomiting and headache dominant)
- Points: GB-20, GB-21, GB-34, SJ-5, GB-41, LV-3

### STOMACH (Wei 胃)

*The Stomach (Yang Earth) receives and ripens food. Its Qi normally descends — rebellious Stomach Qi is one of the most common clinical presentations (nausea, vomiting, hiccough, belching).*

**Stomach Qi Deficiency (Wei Qi Xu)**
- Symptoms: Poor appetite, mild epigastric discomfort (better after eating), fatigue, slight nausea, loose stools
- Distinguished from Spleen Qi deficiency by more gastric focus; often concurrent
- Tongue: Pale, thin white coat
- Pulse: Weak (especially Guan right)
- Formula: Xiang Sha Liu Jun Zi Tang; Si Jun Zi Tang with additions
- Points: ST-36, REN-12, BL-21, SP-3, PC-6

**Stomach Yin Deficiency (Wei Yin Xu)**
- Symptoms: Dry mouth (especially afternoon), slight hunger without desire to eat, epigastric discomfort (dull, burning), hiccough, dry stools, slight nausea
- Tongue: Red with no coat (especially centre — Stomach area), dry; possibly cracked in centre
- Pulse: Thready, slightly rapid
- Formula: Sha Shen Mai Dong Tang; Yi Wei Tang; Zhu Ye Shi Gao Tang (if more Heat)
- Points: ST-36, SP-6, REN-12, KD-3, BL-21, LU-9

**Stomach Fire/Heat (Wei Huo)**
- Symptoms: Intense hunger (can eat frequently without satiety), burning epigastric pain, foul breath, thirst (cold drinks), constipation, bleeding gums, mouth ulcers, vomiting
- Tongue: Red, thick yellow dry coat
- Pulse: Rapid, full, slippery (Guan right)
- Formula: Yu Nu Jian; Qing Wei San; Da Huang Huang Lian Xie Xin Tang (severe)
- Points: ST-44, ST-45, LI-11, SP-6, REN-12, BL-21

**Stomach Cold (Wei Han)**
- Symptoms: Epigastric cold pain (worse cold, better warmth and pressure), nausea, vomiting clear fluid, no thirst, dislike of cold foods
- Tongue: Pale, white coat
- Pulse: Deep, slow, tight or wiry
- Formula: Liang Fu Wan; Li Zhong Wan; Wu Zhu Yu Tang (vomiting dominant)
- Points: REN-12 (moxa), ST-25 (moxa), BL-21, PC-6, ST-36

**Rebellious Stomach Qi (Wei Qi Shang Ni)**
- Stomach Qi failing to descend — a mechanism rather than standalone pattern; determines which formula category to use
- Acute: Hiccough, belching, nausea, vomiting, acid reflux
- Formula: Ban Xia Xie Xin Tang (Damp-Heat + Qi stagnation); Xuan Fu Dai Zhe Tang (chronic Stomach deficiency + Phlegm)
- Points: PC-6 (the single most important point for all nausea), ST-36, REN-12, ST-25

**Food Stagnation in Stomach (Shi Ji Wei)**
- Symptoms: Epigastric fullness and distension (worse after eating), belching (foul-smelling), nausea, vomiting (relieves discomfort), acid regurgitation, foul-smelling loose stools or constipation
- Tongue: Thick greasy coat (white or yellow depending on Heat transformation)
- Pulse: Slippery, full
- Formula: Bao He Wan (mild); Zhi Shi Dao Zhi Wan (more severe with Damp-Heat)
- Points: REN-12, ST-25, ST-44, SJ-6, SP-4, BL-21

### PERICARDIUM (Xin Bao 心包) AND SAN JIAO (三焦)

*The Pericardium is the "Heart Protector" — it screens emotions and pathogens before they reach the Heart. Clinically, Pericardium patterns often mirror Heart patterns but are often more psycho-emotional and less severe. San Jiao is a functional concept governing fluid metabolism and Heat across three body cavities.*

**Pericardium Heat (Xin Bao Re)**
- Pathogen entering the Pericardium (Ying/Blood level warm disease)
- Symptoms: High fever (worse at night), agitation, delirium, confusion, possibly unconsciousness, dry mouth
- Tongue: Deep red, dry, no coat
- Pulse: Rapid, fine
- Formula: Qing Ying Tang; An Gong Niu Huang Wan (unconsciousness); Zi Xue Dan
- Points: PC-9, DU-26, HT-9, LI-11, SP-10, BL-15

**Pericardium Cold (Xin Bao Han)**
- Cold pathogen blocking the Pericardium — rare; most often in Yang deficiency constitutions
- Symptoms: Chest pain (cold, constricting), pallor, cold extremities, sense of dread or terror
- Formula: Su He Xiang Wan
- Points: PC-6, REN-17, HT-7, BL-14 (Back Shu Pericardium), moxa REN-4

**San Jiao — The Three Warmer Patterns:**

*San Jiao is not an organ in the conventional sense — it is the functional space across which Yuan Qi distributes and fluids are metabolised. Clinical patterns often map to the three cavities (Upper, Middle, Lower Jiao).*

**Upper Jiao Phlegm-Heat (Shang Jiao Tan Re)**
- Heart and Lung level — Phlegm-Heat obstructing chest
- Symptoms: Chest oppression, cough with yellow phlegm, palpitations, mental agitation, insomnia
- Formula: Wen Dan Tang + Gua Lou (chest Phlegm); Xiao Xian Xiong Tang
- Points: REN-17, PC-6, LU-1, ST-40, BL-13, BL-14

**Middle Jiao Damp obstruction (Zhong Jiao Shi Zu)**
- Spleen/Stomach level — Damp obstructing the Middle
- Symptoms: Epigastric fullness, nausea, heavy limbs, no appetite, greasy coat
- Formula: Ping Wei San; Huo Xiang Zheng Qi San
- Points: REN-12, SP-9, ST-40, BL-20, BL-21

**Lower Jiao Damp-Heat (Xia Jiao Shi Re)**
- Kidney/Bladder/Intestine level — Damp-Heat descending
- Symptoms: Urinary frequency/urgency/burning, dark cloudy urine, vaginal discharge (yellow), diarrhoea (urgent, smelly), lower abdominal pain/heat
- Formula: Ba Zheng San (urinary); Long Dan Xie Gan Tang (Liver/Gallbladder route); Yi Yi Fu Zi Bai Jiang San (intestinal Damp-Heat with Cold)
- Points: SP-9, BL-28, REN-3, BL-22, KD-7, LI-11

## 3. SIX STAGE DIFFERENTIATION (SHANG HAN LUN 傷寒論)

Zhang Zhongjing's framework for Cold pathogen progression. Each stage has a principle formula (the classical core formulas of Chinese medicine).

| Stage | Location | Clinical Features | Principle Formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tai Yang (Greater Yang)** | Exterior — Wei level | Chills, fever, headache, stiff neck, floating pulse | Ma Huang Tang / Gui Zhi Tang |
| **Yang Ming (Bright Yang)** | Interior — Yang organs | High fever, sweating, thirst, constipation OR dry stool, big full pulse | Bai Hu Tang / Da/Xiao Cheng Qi Tang |
| **Shao Yang (Lesser Yang)** | Half-interior/exterior | Alternating chills/fever, bitter taste, nausea, hypochondriac fullness, wiry pulse | Xiao Chai Hu Tang |
| **Tai Yin (Greater Yin)** | Interior — Spleen | No appetite, vomiting, abdominal pain (likes pressure), no thirst, diarrhoea | Li Zhong Wan |
| **Shao Yin (Lesser Yin)** | Heart/Kidney | Cold: Exhaustion, cold body, desire for sleep, deep faint pulse. OR Hot: Restlessness, insomnia | Fu Zi Tang (cold) / Huang Lian E Jiao Tang (hot) |
| **Jue Yin (Terminal Yin)** | Liver/Pericardium | Complex mixed: cold limbs with heat above, thirst, hunger but vomiting if eats, wasting and thirsting | Wu Mei Wan |

## 4. FOUR LEVEL DIFFERENTIATION (WEN BING 溫病)

Ye Tianshi's framework for warm/heat pathogen diseases.

| Level | Location | Clinical Features | Treatment Principle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wei (Defensive)** | Exterior — similar to Tai Yang but Hot | Fever > chills, slight sweating, sore throat, floating rapid pulse | Disperse Wind-Heat; vent exterior |
| **Qi (Qi Level)** | Interior — non-nutritive | High fever, no chills, sweating, thirst, restlessness, rapid pulse | Clear Heat; preserve fluids |
| **Ying (Nutritive)** | Interior — deep; begins to affect Blood | Fever worse at night, restlessness, dry mouth without desire to drink, faint rash, possibly delirium | Clear Nutritive Heat; cool Blood |
| **Xue (Blood Level)** | Deepest — Blood level damage | Haemorrhage (purpura, bleeding), delirium, convulsions, very red or purple tongue | Cool Blood; stop bleeding; extinguish Wind |

## 5. SAN JIAO (TRIPLE WARMER) DIFFERENTIATION — ADDITIONAL FRAMEWORK

The San Jiao framework (from the Wen Bing school — Ye Tianshi and Wu Jutong) divides complex systemic disease by body cavity. Used especially for epidemic/warm diseases and complex fluid-metabolism disorders.

**Upper Jiao (Shang Jiao 上焦)** — Heart and Lung
- Pathogen enters via mouth/nose; first affects Lung (Wei level)
- Symptoms: Fever, chills, headache, sore throat, cough — exterior to interior progression
- Principle: "Treat the Upper Jiao like a feather — light, floating, dispersing herbs"
- Formulas: Yin Qiao San, Sang Ju Yin (early); Qing Ying Tang (deeper Heat)

**Middle Jiao (Zhong Jiao 中焦)** — Spleen and Stomach
- Pathogen in Spleen-Stomach; both Damp-Heat and Dry-Heat variants
- Damp-Heat type: Fever that doesn't reduce with sweating, heavy feeling, greasy coat, no thirst
- Dry-Heat type (Yang Ming): High fever, thirst, constipation, dry coat
- Principle: "Treat the Middle Jiao like balance scales — clear and regulate"
- Formulas: Bai Hu Tang (Dry-Heat); San Ren Tang or Huo Pu Xia Ling Tang (Damp-Heat)

**Lower Jiao (Xia Jiao 下焦)** — Liver and Kidney
- Pathogen has consumed Liver and Kidney Yin; late-stage disease
- Symptoms: Low-grade fever (worse at night), night sweats, dry mouth, tremors, convulsions (Liver Wind from Yin deficiency), fatigue, deep red tongue no coat
- Principle: "Treat the Lower Jiao like a heavy weight — nourishing, consolidating herbs"
- Formulas: Jia Jian Fu Mai Tang; San Jia Fu Mai Tang (Liver Wind concurrent); Zeng Ye Tang (fluids depleted)

**Clinical value of the San Jiao framework:**
- Maps disease progression spatially (top → middle → bottom)
- Guides prognosis: Upper Jiao = mild; Lower Jiao = serious
- Helps sequence treatment in complex systemic conditions
- Especially useful for: post-viral fatigue, Long COVID patterns, complex autoimmune conditions with multi-system involvement

## 6. QI, BLOOD, AND FLUID PATHOLOGY

### Qi Pathology
- **Qi Deficiency:** Fatigue, weak function, prolapse tendency, poor immunity
- **Qi Stagnation:** Pain (distending, moving), emotional constraint, functional disruption without organic pathology
- **Qi Rebellion (counterflow):** Qi moving wrong direction — nausea/vomiting (Stomach Qi rebels upward), cough/asthma (Lung Qi rebels upward), headache (Liver Yang rising)
- **Qi Collapse (Qi Xian):** Acute depletion — syncope, shock, organ prolapse crisis

### Blood Pathology
- **Blood Deficiency:** Pale complexion/nails/tongue/lips, dizziness, poor memory, insomnia, scanty periods, numbness, dry skin
- **Blood Stasis (Yu Xue):** Fixed sharp pain (worse pressure), dark complexion, purple tongue or petechiae, choppy pulse, dark menstrual blood with clots, varicosities, masses
- **Blood Heat:** Bleeding (red/dark), skin eruptions, restlessness, rapid full pulse — often Heat pathogen entering Blood level
- **Blood Cold:** Pain improved by warmth, cold extremities, pale/purple menstrual blood

### Fluid Pathology
- **Damp:** Heavy, soggy feeling, greasy coat, slippery pulse, swelling — obstructs Qi flow
- **Phlegm (Tan):** Thick Damp — can obstruct anywhere: Heart (mental confusion), Lung (cough), channels (nodules), head (dizziness)
- **Fluid Retention (Shui):** Oedema, accumulation in body cavities — Spleen/Kidney Yang failure
- **Dry (Zao):** Fluid deficiency — dry skin, mucous membranes, stools — often Yin deficiency or late-stage Heat pathogen
