## The Architecture of TCM Clinical Thinking

TCM is not a single system. It is a family of interrelated diagnostic and therapeutic frameworks, each with distinct strengths. Before any clinical response, mentally identify which framework is primary for this case:

| Framework | Best For | Core Logic |
|---|---|---|
| **Zang-Fu / Eight Principles** | Internal medicine, chronic conditions | Organ system disharmony expressed through Yin/Yang, Hot/Cold, Excess/Deficiency, Interior/Exterior |
| **Six Stage (Shang Han)** | Acute febrile illness, cold invasion patterns | Zhang Zhongjing's pathogen-penetration model through 6 energetic layers |
| **Four Level (Wen Bing)** | Warm/heat pathogen disease, epidemic disease | Ye Tianshi's heat transformation through Wei/Qi/Ying/Xue levels |
| **Five Elements (Wu Xing)** | Psycho-emotional, constitutional, long-cycle imbalances | Inter-organ relationships via generating/controlling cycles |
| **Eight Extraordinary Vessels** | Deep constitutional work, gynecology, extraordinary presentations | Pre-heaven Jing-level channels that regulate all 12 primary meridians |
| **San Jiao (Triple Warmer)** | Fluid metabolism, complex systemic patterns | Fluid transformation across three body cavities |
| **Channel Theory** | Acupuncture point selection, musculo-skeletal, pain | Qi flow through 12 primary + 8 extraordinary + divergent + sinew channels |

*A master clinician integrates multiple frameworks simultaneously, the way a chess player sees the board from multiple dimensions at once.*

## Diagnostic Framework

### Step 1: The Four Examinations (Si Zhen 四診)

**Inspection (Wang 望)**
- Complexion: pallor (Qi/Blood deficiency), redness (Heat), sallow/yellow (Spleen Qi deficiency), dark (Kidney deficiency, Blood stasis)
- Tongue body: pale (Cold/Deficiency), red (Heat/Yin deficiency), purple (Blood stasis), swollen/teeth-marked (Spleen Qi/Yang deficiency), thin (Blood/Yin deficiency)
- Tongue coat: white thin (normal/Cold), white thick (Cold-Damp), yellow thin (mild Heat), yellow thick (Heat-Damp/Phlegm-Heat), absent coat (Yin deficiency)
- Eyes, lips, nails, skin, posture, spirit (Shen 神) — the master diagnostic variable

**Auscultation/Olfaction (Wen 聞)**
- Voice strength: weak = Deficiency; loud = Excess
- Breathing: shallow = Lung Qi deficiency; laboured = Lung/Kidney failure to grasp Qi
- Body odour: rancid/sour = Liver; burnt = Heart; sweet/fetid = Spleen; fishy = Lung; putrid = Kidney (classical associations, clinically guide not determine)

**Inquiry (Wen 問)** — The Ten Questions (Shi Wen 十問)
1. Chills/fever patterns
2. Sweating (spontaneous = Qi deficiency; night sweats = Yin deficiency)
3. Head/body sensations
4. Urination/defaecation (key Qi and fluid metabolism indicators)
5. Food/appetite/thirst (Spleen/Stomach Qi; fluid metabolism)
6. Chest/abdomen sensations
7. Hearing/vision
8. Sleep patterns
9. Pain characteristics (dull = Deficiency; sharp fixed = Stasis; moving = Qi stagnation; heavy = Damp)
10. Gynaecological / emotional history

**Palpation (Qie 切)** — Pulse Diagnosis (Mai Zhen 脈診)
- Location: superficial (exterior patterns); deep (interior patterns)
- Rate: rapid >90 (Heat); slow <60 (Cold/Deficiency)
- Force: strong (Excess); weak (Deficiency)
- Width: flooding (Heat); thin/thready (Blood/Yin deficiency); wiry (Liver)
- Depth: floating (Wei Qi, exterior); sinking (interior)
- Key pulse types: Wiry/Taut (Liver Qi; pain); Slippery (Phlegm; pregnancy; Excess); Choppy (Blood deficiency; stasis); Tight (Cold; pain); Soggy (Dampness; Deficiency)

*Three positions each wrist: Cun (distal), Guan (middle), Chi (proximal) — each reflecting different organ systems*

## Treatment Modalities

### 1. Herbal Medicine (Zhong Yao 中藥)

**Approach:** Always prescribe formulas, not single herbs (classical principle: single herbs are for emergency/targeted use; formulas create balanced therapeutic environments).

**Formula construction principles:**
- **Jun (君)** — Emperor herb: main therapeutic action
- **Chen (臣)** — Minister herb: supports and enhances Jun
- **Zuo (佐)** — Assistant herb: addresses secondary symptoms; moderates harshness
- **Shi (使)** — Envoy herb: directs formula to target organ/meridian; harmonises

**Read `/references/herbal-medicine.md` for formula library, modification principles, and modern pharmacological correlates.**

**Key clinical considerations:**
- Contraindications in pregnancy (herbs that move Blood strongly)
- Drug-herb interactions (particularly anticoagulants, immunosuppressants, digoxin)
- Quality and sourcing concerns (heavy metal contamination in some commercial preparations)
- SA-specific availability: some raw herbs unavailable; granule/patent alternatives

### 2. Acupuncture (Zhen Jiu 針灸)

**Point selection strategy:**
- **Local points** — address site of pathology
- **Distal points** — channel theory; strong therapeutic action from a distance
- **Symptomatic points** — empirical points for specific symptoms
- **Constitutional points** — address root pattern (often more important than branch)

**Read `/references/acupuncture-channels.md` for channel pathways, point functions, and needling depth/direction.**

### 3. Tui Na (推拿) — Chinese Medical Massage

Channel-based manual therapy. Distinct from Western massage — uses specific techniques (Gun fa rolling, An pressing, Na grasping, Mo rubbing) to regulate Qi in channels.

### 4. Medical Qigong Prescription (Yi Gong 醫功)

Prescribe specific Qigong sequences/exercises as therapeutic intervention based on pattern:
- Spleen/Stomach Qi deficiency → Ba Duan Jin (8 Brocades) — Earth-centring sequences
- Liver Qi stagnation → Standing meditation with lateral rib expansion; lateral bending
- Kidney deficiency → Zhan Zhuang root cultivation; kidney-tapping sequence
- Lung Qi deficiency → Six Healing Sounds (Liu Zi Jue) — specifically 'Si' sound

### 5. Dietary Therapy (Shi Liao 食療)

Food as medicine. Every food has thermal nature (hot/warm/neutral/cool/cold), flavour (five flavours = five organ tropism), and channel entry. Prescribe dietary modifications as part of every treatment plan.

**Core dietary principles:**
- Match food thermals to pattern (Cold patterns: warming foods; Heat patterns: cooling foods)
- Five flavours: sour → Liver; bitter → Heart; sweet → Spleen; pungent/acrid → Lung; salty → Kidney
- Cooked vs raw: Spleen Qi deficiency → cooked food always; Heat excess → raw/cooling acceptable

## Modern Integration Framework

**Position:** TCM and biomedicine describe the same biological reality through different observational lenses. Neither is complete alone. The integrated practitioner uses both without distorting either.

**Read `/references/modern-integration.md` for biomedical correlates, research evidence, and integration protocols.**

**Core integration principles:**
1. TCM pattern → biomedical mechanism hypothesis (e.g., Kidney Yin deficiency → HPA axis dysregulation, cortisol pattern, thyroid, sex hormone changes)
2. Biomedical investigation informs TCM management (blood tests narrow differential)
3. Drug-herb interaction awareness is non-negotiable
4. Evidence hierarchy: Cochrane reviews and RCTs → case series → classical clinical tradition → single case reports

**Where evidence is strong:**
- Acupuncture: chronic pain (strong), chemotherapy-induced nausea (strong), fertility support (moderate)
- Herbal medicine: specific formulas for IBS, menopausal symptoms, hepatitis support (moderate to strong for specific conditions)
- Mind-body: Qigong for hypertension, cancer rehabilitation (moderate)

**Where evidence is weak or absent:** Many classical indications lack RCT evidence. This does NOT mean they are ineffective — it means they are understudied. State this distinction explicitly.

## The Five Element System — Clinical Application

Five Elements are not separate from Zang-Fu — they are the *relational* layer showing how organs affect each other.

```
        FIRE (Heart / Small Intestine / Pericardium / San Jiao)
              ↑ generates
WOOD ————————————————→ EARTH (Spleen / Stomach)
(Liver/GB)     ↑ controls ↓ generates        ↓ generates
              WATER ←———————————— METAL
           (Kidney/Bladder)    (Lung/LI)
```

**Generating cycle (Sheng 生):** Wood → Fire → Earth → Metal → Water → Wood
**Controlling cycle (Ke 克):** Wood controls Earth; Earth controls Water; Water controls Fire; Fire controls Metal; Metal controls Wood

**Clinical application:**
- Over-acting (Cheng 乘): One element over-controls another due to excess (e.g., Wood/Liver over-controlling Earth/Spleen → digestive problems from stress)
- Insulting (Wu 侮): Reverse controlling — a weak element fails to be controlled (e.g., Metal/Lung fails to control Wood/Liver → chronic Liver excess with weak Lung)
- Mother-child relationships: Deficiency often requires tonifying the mother (e.g., Liver Blood deficiency → tonify Kidney, the mother of Liver in generating cycle)

## Psycho-Emotional Medicine in TCM

Every organ has an associated emotion. Emotion causes organ dysfunction; organ dysfunction generates emotional patterns. This is bidirectional — never assume one-way causation.

| Organ | Healthy Emotion | Pathological Excess | Deficiency Expression |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heart | Joy, presence | Mania, restless laughing | Joylessness, disconnection |
| Liver | Decisiveness, vision | Anger, frustration, rigidity | Indecisiveness, depression, timidity |
| Spleen | Groundedness, thinking | Obsession, rumination, worry | Scattered thinking, poor memory |
| Lung | Letting go, grief | Prolonged grief, melancholy | Inability to feel loss |
| Kidney | Will, determination | Fear, existential dread | Loss of will, phobia, shock sequelae |

**Shen (神) disturbance patterns:**
- Shen Restlessness (Heart Fire or Heart Blood deficiency)
- Shen Obstruction (Phlegm-Fire or Phlegm misting Heart)
- Shen Collapse (severe Deficiency — Qi and Yang)
